Home Insurance in Vermont: What You Need to Know

Vermont homeowners face significant risk from winter weather and flooding — Tropical Storm Irene (2011) caused catastrophic flooding that washed out roads and damaged thousands of homes across the state. The 2023 Vermont floods caused over $500 million in damage, striking communities that had barely recovered from Irene. Standard policies cover wind and snow damage but not flooding.

Average Homeowners Insurance Costs in Vermont

Homeowners in Vermont typically pay approximately $1,020 per year ($85/month) for homeowners insurance in 2026, based on industry average data for a home with approximately $290,000 in dwelling coverage.

Natural Disaster Risk in Vermont

Primary risks: Flooding (high), Winter storms (very high), Nor'easter (high), Wildfire (low)

Vermont's river valleys face severe flash flooding risk — narrow valleys can experience rapid water rise from upstream rainfall. Montpelier has flooded catastrophically twice in recent memory. Nor'easters and winter storms deposit heavy snow that can cause roof failure in older structures. Ice dams are common in winter.

What Makes Vermont Unique

Vermont's 2023 floods — which struck in July — were a stark reminder that inland flooding risk is severe even in a small, landlocked state far from the Gulf Coast. Many Vermont homeowners who had gone without flood insurance since Irene found themselves facing the same uninsured losses a second time. Vermont's mountain topography channels water into narrow valleys where flooding can be extremely rapid.

What Vermont Homeowners Pay per $1,000 of Coverage

Dividing our Vermont estimate of $1,020 a year by the typical dwelling coverage of $290,000 gives about $3.52 per $1,000 of coverage — below the national middle rate among the 50 states in our estimates (median $5.17).

A low rate per $1,000 means the headline premium mostly reflects home values rather than unusual risk; the biggest savings levers are usually the deductible and bundling.

If Standard Insurers Decline You in Vermont

Vermont is one of the states without a FAIR plan or other state-created insurer of last resort for homes. If standard insurers decline you, the usual route is the surplus lines market through a licensed surplus lines broker. Those insurers can cover risks others won’t, but their rates and policy forms aren’t approved by the state and their policies are generally not backed by the state guaranty fund, so check the insurer’s financial rating before buying.

How to Save on Home Insurance in Vermont

Vermont homeowners in river valleys and low-lying areas should obtain flood insurance — the state has experienced two major flood disasters in 12 years. Montpelier, which flooded severely in both 2011 and 2023, is a vivid example of the risk. Adequate snow load coverage is important for older Vermont homes with heavy timber framing.

  • Standard policies exclude flood damage regardless of cause — given Vermont's flood risk, a separate NFIP or private flood policy is worth pricing even outside a mapped flood zone.

Frequently Asked Questions

What does home insurance cost per $1,000 of coverage in Vermont?

Based on our estimates of $1,020 a year for typical dwelling coverage of $290,000, Vermont homeowners pay about $3.52 per $1,000 of coverage.

Does Vermont have a FAIR plan for homeowners?

No. Vermont has no FAIR plan or other state-created insurer of last resort for homes; homeowners declined by standard insurers usually turn to the surplus lines market.
