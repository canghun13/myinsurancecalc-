Home Insurance in Mississippi: What You Need to Know

Mississippi homeowners face severe risk from multiple perils. Coastal Mississippi (Harrison, Hancock, Jackson counties) experienced catastrophic destruction from Hurricane Katrina (2005) and faces ongoing hurricane wind and storm surge risk. Inland Mississippi has very high tornado frequency — the state is in the heart of Dixie Alley. Flooding is common along the Mississippi River. The state wind pool provides coverage in coastal counties.

Average Homeowners Insurance Costs in Mississippi

Homeowners in Mississippi typically pay approximately $1,940 per year ($162/month) for homeowners insurance in 2026, based on industry average data for a home with approximately $170,000 in dwelling coverage.

Natural Disaster Risk in Mississippi

Primary risks: Hurricane (very high, coastal), Tornado (very high), Flooding (high), Hail (moderate)

Coastal Mississippi faces extreme hurricane wind and storm surge risk — the Gulf Coast was devastated by Katrina in 2005 and faces recurring hurricane threats. Inland Mississippi has some of the highest tornado rates in the nation, particularly in Dixie Alley through the center of the state. Flooding along the Mississippi River affects western Mississippi counties.

What Makes Mississippi Unique

Hurricane Katrina's surge destruction of coastal Mississippi exceeded the flooding damage in New Orleans in many respects — entire communities in Hancock County were obliterated by 30+ foot storm surges. The destruction exposed the critical difference between wind coverage (homeowners policy) and surge coverage (flood insurance) that many coastal homeowners did not understand until they filed claims.

Last Resort Coverage Options in Mississippi

Wind Pool / Beach Plan: Mississippi operates a state wind pool or beach plan that provides windstorm coverage for coastal properties that cannot obtain it in the standard market. This coverage is typically purchased separately from standard homeowners insurance and covers wind damage while the standard policy covers other perils. Coastal homeowners in Mississippi may need both policies for complete protection.

What Mississippi Homeowners Pay per $1,000 of Coverage

Dividing our Mississippi estimate of $1,940 a year by the typical dwelling coverage of $170,000 gives about $11.41 per $1,000 of coverage — one of the ten highest rate among the 50 states in our estimates (median $5.17).

A high rate per $1,000 signals that weather and claims risk, not home values, is driving premiums — so deductible choices, roof condition, and mitigation discounts matter more here than in most states.

If Standard Insurers Decline You in Mississippi

Mississippi’s insurer of last resort is the Mississippi Windstorm Underwriting Association (coastal wind).

Hurricane Deductibles in Mississippi

Mississippi is one of 19 states, plus Washington, D.C., where homeowners policies can carry a separate hurricane or named-storm deductible. Instead of a flat dollar amount, it’s usually a percentage of the dwelling coverage — typically 1% to 5%, sometimes higher on the coast. On the typical Mississippi home insured for $170,000, a 2% hurricane deductible is $3,400 and a 5% deductible is $8,500, compared with a standard $1,000 all-perils deductible. Check which applies, and what triggers it, before storm season. Flood damage is excluded either way and needs a separate flood policy.

How to Save on Home Insurance in Mississippi

Coastal Mississippi homeowners must have both homeowners (wind) and flood insurance — these cover different and complementary perils. Wind mitigation inspections can reduce premiums for properties with qualifying storm-resistant features. Inland homeowners should prioritize tornado coverage and verify replacement cost provisions.

  • Given Mississippi's hurricane exposure, check whether your policy has a separate hurricane deductible (often 1-5% of dwelling coverage) and confirm wind coverage isn't carved out into a separate policy.
