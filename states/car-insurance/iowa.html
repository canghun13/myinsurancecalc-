Minimum Coverage Requirements

Iowa requires drivers to carry minimum liability insurance of 20/40/15 — meaning $20,000 per person for bodily injury, $40,000 per accident for bodily injury, and $15,000 for property damage.

State Regulations and Key Rules

Iowa is an at-fault state with relatively low minimum requirements. Uninsured motorist coverage must be offered but is not required. Iowa has one of the lowest uninsured driver rates in the country.

What Makes Iowa Unique

Iowa's rural landscape and low traffic congestion result in some of the most affordable car insurance rates in the Midwest. Hail and deer collisions are common comprehensive claims.

Uninsured Drivers in Iowa

Approximately 9.0% of drivers in Iowa are estimated to be uninsured.

Risk Factors Specific to Iowa

Iowa experiences some of the highest hail damage rates in the nation during spring and summer — comprehensive coverage is highly advisable. Deer collisions are extremely common, particularly in October and November. Spring flooding periodically affects roadways across the state.

What Iowa’s Minimum Actually Covers

Iowa’s minimum is 20/40/15: $20,000 per injured person, $40,000 total per accident for injuries, and $15,000 for property damage. With new vehicles averaging roughly $50,000, the $15,000 property damage limit would leave about $35,000 unpaid if you totaled an average new car — and that gap becomes your personal debt. On the injury side, a single hospital admission after a serious crash can exceed $20,000 quickly; anything above the limit can be pursued from your assets and future wages.

No-Fault, PIP, and Required Add-Ons in Iowa

Uninsured motorist coverage isn’t mandatory in Iowa, though insurers generally must offer it and you may have to reject it in writing.

How Iowa Compares: Uninsured Drivers and Price

An estimated 9.0% of Iowa drivers are uninsured — roughly 1 in 11 — a middle-of-the-pack uninsured rate.

Our average premium estimate of $1,040 a year puts Iowa among the ten least expensive states in our 50-state estimates (median $1,395).

How to Save on Car Insurance in Iowa

Iowa's already competitive rates can be reduced further through multi-policy bundling. Farmers and rural drivers should ask about farm vehicle discounts available through regional carriers. Paying premiums annually rather than monthly avoids installment fees that can add up over time.

  • Iowa's minimum liability requirement of 20/40/15 is the legal floor, not a safe target — since PIP isn't required in Iowa, a separate medical payments (MedPay) add-on is worth pricing out.
  • With an estimated 9.0% of Iowa drivers uninsured, carrying uninsured/underinsured motorist coverage above the state minimum is one of the highest-value add-ons you can buy.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the minimum car insurance in Iowa?

20/40/15: $20,000 of bodily injury liability per person, $40,000 per accident, and $15,000 of property damage liability.

Is Iowa a no-fault state?

No. Iowa is an at-fault state, so the driver who causes an accident is responsible for the resulting injuries and damage, usually through their liability insurance.

How many drivers are uninsured in Iowa?

An estimated 9.0% of Iowa drivers are uninsured, roughly 1 in 11.
