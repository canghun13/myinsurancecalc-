Minimum Coverage Requirements

Colorado requires drivers to carry minimum liability insurance of 25/50/15 — meaning $25,000 per person for bodily injury, $50,000 per accident for bodily injury, and $15,000 for property damage. PIP coverage is not required in Colorado, though it may be available as an optional add-on. These are the legal minimums; most insurance professionals recommend carrying significantly higher limits to protect personal assets in a serious accident. A common recommendation is at least 100/300/100, which provides substantially more protection without a proportionally large premium increase.

Average Car Insurance Costs in Colorado

Drivers in Colorado typically pay approximately $1,740 per year ($145/month) for car insurance, based on 2026 industry average data. Individual premiums vary significantly based on driving record, age, vehicle type, annual mileage, and selected coverage levels. Drivers with clean records may see rates well below the state average, while those with violations, claims, or high-risk profiles may pay substantially more.

State Regulations and Key Rules

Colorado is an at-fault state. Uninsured and underinsured motorist coverage must be offered but can be declined in writing. Medical payments (MedPay) coverage is also required to be offered.

What Makes Colorado Unique

Colorado's hailstorms are among the most severe in the nation, frequently causing extensive vehicle damage. Comprehensive coverage is strongly recommended, particularly on the Front Range.

Uninsured Drivers in Colorado

Approximately 16.3% of drivers in Colorado are estimated to be uninsured. Carrying uninsured and underinsured motorist coverage provides important protection against the financial impact of an accident with an uninsured or underinsured driver. Uninsured motorist (UM) coverage pays for your medical expenses and vehicle damage if you are hit by a driver without insurance or with insufficient coverage. Underinsured motorist (UIM) coverage fills the gap when the at-fault driver's policy limits are too low to cover your losses.

Risk Factors Specific to Colorado

The Denver and Front Range area has some of the highest hail claim rates in the United States. Mountain driving in winter requires adequate collision coverage. Black ice and sudden spring snowstorms present year-round hazards.

What Colorado’s Minimum Actually Covers

Colorado’s minimum is 25/50/15: $25,000 per injured person, $50,000 total per accident for injuries, and $15,000 for property damage. With new vehicles averaging roughly $50,000, the $15,000 property damage limit would leave about $35,000 unpaid if you totaled an average new car — and that gap becomes your personal debt. On the injury side, a single hospital admission after a serious crash can exceed $25,000 quickly; anything above the limit can be pursued from your assets and future wages.

No-Fault, PIP, and Required Add-Ons in Colorado

Colorado is an at-fault state: the driver who causes a crash is responsible for the other party’s injuries and damage, usually through their liability insurance. PIP isn’t required, so your own injuries are covered by your health insurance, optional medical payments coverage, or a claim against the other driver. Uninsured motorist coverage isn’t mandatory in Colorado, though insurers generally must offer it and you may have to reject it in writing.

How Colorado Compares: Uninsured Drivers and Price

An estimated 16.3% of Colorado drivers are uninsured — roughly 1 in 6 — a middle-of-the-pack uninsured rate. At that rate, uninsured motorist coverage is one of the most valuable add-ons you can buy.

Our average premium estimate of $1,740 a year puts Colorado above the national middle in our 50-state estimates (median $1,395). Your own price depends far more on your record, age, vehicle, and ZIP code than on the statewide average.

How to Save on Car Insurance in Colorado

Comprehensive coverage is essential in Colorado given hail risk — but you can manage costs by choosing a higher deductible. Denver metro drivers benefit from comparing quotes annually as competition among insurers is strong. Garaging a vehicle rather than parking on the street reduces both theft and hail exposure.

  • Compare quotes from at least 3–5 insurers annually — rates for identical coverage can vary by hundreds of dollars.
  • Colorado's minimum liability requirement of 25/50/15 is the legal floor, not a safe target — since PIP isn't required in Colorado, a separate medical payments (MedPay) add-on is worth pricing out. Most agents recommend at least 100/300/100 for meaningful asset protection.
  • Ask about bundling discounts if you also need home or renters insurance.
  • With an estimated 16.3% of Colorado drivers uninsured, carrying uninsured/underinsured motorist coverage above the state minimum is one of the highest-value add-ons you can buy.
  • Review your coverage annually, especially after major life changes: moving, buying a new vehicle, or adding a teen driver.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is Colorado a no-fault state?
