Minimum Coverage Requirements

Rhode Island requires drivers to carry minimum liability insurance of 25/50/25 — meaning $25,000 per person for bodily injury, $50,000 per accident for bodily injury, and $25,000 for property damage.

Average Car Insurance Costs in Rhode Island

Drivers in Rhode Island typically pay approximately $1,890 per year ($158/month) for car insurance, based on 2026 industry average data.

State Regulations and Key Rules

Rhode Island is an at-fault state. Uninsured motorist coverage must be offered but is not required. Despite being the smallest state, Rhode Island has above-average premiums driven by high traffic density and urban congestion.

What Makes Rhode Island Unique

Rhode Island's high population density and urban driving environment contribute to above-average accident rates and insurance costs despite the state's small geographic size.

Uninsured Drivers in Rhode Island

Approximately 16.5% of drivers in Rhode Island are estimated to be uninsured.

Risk Factors Specific to Rhode Island

Rhode Island's coastal areas face nor'easter flooding and storm surge risk. Providence traffic density produces high accident frequency. New England winter storms cause significant collision claims across the state.

What Rhode Island’s Minimum Actually Covers

Rhode Island’s minimum is 25/50/25: $25,000 per injured person, $50,000 total per accident for injuries, and $25,000 for property damage. With new vehicles averaging roughly $50,000, the $25,000 property damage limit would leave about $25,000 unpaid if you totaled an average new car — and that gap becomes your personal debt.

No-Fault, PIP, and Required Add-Ons in Rhode Island

Uninsured motorist coverage isn’t mandatory in Rhode Island, though insurers generally must offer it and you may have to reject it in writing.

How Rhode Island Compares: Uninsured Drivers and Price

An estimated 16.5% of Rhode Island drivers are uninsured — roughly 1 in 6 — one of the highest uninsured rates in the country. At that rate, uninsured motorist coverage is one of the most valuable add-ons you can buy.

Our average premium estimate of $1,890 a year puts Rhode Island among the ten most expensive states in our 50-state estimates (median $1,395).

How to Save on Car Insurance in Rhode Island

Rhode Island's small size means rates are fairly consistent statewide, but urban areas like Providence still carry a premium. Comparison shopping is particularly important given above-average baseline rates. Bundling home and auto with regional New England insurers is often the most effective discount.

  • Rhode Island's minimum liability requirement of 25/50/25 is the legal floor, not a safe target — since PIP isn't required in Rhode Island, a separate medical payments (MedPay) add-on is worth pricing out.
  • With an estimated 16.5% of Rhode Island drivers uninsured, carrying uninsured/underinsured motorist coverage above the state minimum is one of the highest-value add-ons you can buy.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the minimum car insurance in Rhode Island?

25/50/25: $25,000 of bodily injury liability per person, $50,000 per accident, and $25,000 of property damage liability.

Is Rhode Island a no-fault state?

No. Rhode Island is an at-fault state, so the driver who causes an accident is responsible for the resulting injuries and damage, usually through their liability insurance.

How many drivers are uninsured in Rhode Island?

An estimated 16.5% of Rhode Island drivers are uninsured, roughly 1 in 6.
