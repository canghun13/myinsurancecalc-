Minimum Coverage Requirements

North Carolina requires drivers to carry minimum liability insurance of 50/100/50 — meaning $50,000 per person for bodily injury, $100,000 per accident for bodily injury, and $50,000 for property damage. These limits took effect effective July 1, 2025; older guides that list 30/60/25 are out of date.

State Regulations and Key Rules

North Carolina is an at-fault state with a state-managed reinsurance facility that helps insure high-risk drivers. Uninsured motorist coverage is required. The state uses a contributory negligence rule — any fault can bar recovery.

What Makes North Carolina Unique

North Carolina's contributory negligence rule means that even 1% fault can prevent a driver from recovering damages. This strict standard, combined with the state's mandatory uninsured motorist requirement, shapes the local insurance market.

Uninsured Drivers in North Carolina

Risk Factors Specific to North Carolina

North Carolina's coastal areas face significant hurricane risk, particularly Wilmington and the Outer Banks. The western mountains see ice storms and heavy snow. Deer collisions are common statewide in fall months.

What North Carolina’s Minimum Actually Covers

North Carolina’s minimum is 50/100/50: $50,000 per injured person, $100,000 total per accident for injuries, and $50,000 for property damage. These limits took effect July 1, 2025 (from 30/60/25). At $50,000, North Carolina’s property damage minimum is high enough to cover the average new vehicle, roughly $50,000, which is unusual. On the injury side, a single hospital admission after a serious crash can exceed $50,000 quickly; anything above the limit can be pursued from your assets and future wages.

No-Fault, PIP, and Required Add-Ons in North Carolina

North Carolina also requires UM and UIM coverage, which pays when the at-fault driver has no insurance or too little.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the minimum car insurance in North Carolina?

50/100/50: $50,000 of bodily injury liability per person, $100,000 per accident, and $50,000 of property damage liability. These limits took effect July 1, 2025 (from 30/60/25).

Is North Carolina a no-fault state?

No. North Carolina is an at-fault state, so the driver who causes an accident is responsible for the resulting injuries and damage, usually through their liability insurance.

Is uninsured motorist coverage required in North Carolina?

Yes. North Carolina requires UM and UIM coverage.

How many drivers are uninsured in North Carolina?

An estimated 10.6% of North Carolina drivers are uninsured, roughly 1 in 9.
