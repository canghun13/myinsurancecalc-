Minimum Coverage Requirements

New Jersey requires drivers to carry minimum liability insurance of 35/70/25 — meaning $35,000 per person for bodily injury, $70,000 per accident for bodily injury, and $25,000 for property damage. These limits took effect for standard policies effective January 1, 2026; older guides that list 15/30/5 are out of date. Personal Injury Protection (PIP) is also required, covering medical expenses regardless of fault.

State Regulations and Key Rules

New Jersey is a no-fault state offering two policy options: Basic (minimal coverage, no bodily injury liability required) and Standard (full coverage options). PIP is required under both options. New Jersey has some of the most complex insurance laws in the country.

What Makes New Jersey Unique

New Jersey's two-tier policy system (Basic vs Standard) creates significant coverage differences. The Basic policy is cheaper but leaves drivers exposed to major liability. Most financial advisors recommend the Standard policy for adequate protection.

Uninsured Drivers in New Jersey

Risk Factors Specific to New Jersey

Northern New Jersey's dense urban areas and proximity to New York City produce extremely high accident and theft rates. Coastal New Jersey faces significant flooding risk from nor'easters and tropical storms. I-95 and the Turnpike see high accident frequency.

What New Jersey’s Minimum Actually Covers

New Jersey’s minimum is 35/70/25: $35,000 per injured person, $70,000 total per accident for injuries, and $25,000 for property damage. These limits took effect January 1, 2026 for standard policies (from 25/50/25). With new vehicles averaging roughly $50,000, the $25,000 property damage limit would leave about $25,000 unpaid if you totaled an average new car — and that gap becomes your personal debt. On the injury side, a single hospital admission after a serious crash can exceed $35,000 quickly; anything above the limit can be pursued from your assets and future wages.

No-Fault, PIP, and Required Add-Ons in New Jersey

New Jersey is a choice no-fault state. Drivers carry PIP of $250,000 by default, with options as low as $15,000. The choice you make affects whether you can sue for pain and suffering after a crash, so it is worth reading rather than accepting the default. New Jersey also requires UM/UIM coverage, which pays when the at-fault driver has no insurance or too little.

How New Jersey Compares: Uninsured Drivers and Price

An estimated 14.9% of New Jersey drivers are uninsured — roughly 1 in 7 — a middle-of-the-pack uninsured rate. At that rate, uninsured motorist coverage is one of the most valuable add-ons you can buy.

Our average premium estimate of $1,950 a year puts New Jersey among the ten most expensive states in our 50-state estimates (median $1,395).

How to Save on Car Insurance in New Jersey

Northern New Jersey cities have some of the highest rates in the state — drivers in South Jersey and suburban areas pay significantly less. New Jersey's two-policy system means understanding the Standard vs Basic tradeoff is essential. Telematics programs are widely available and suit the many NJ residents with short or commuter-friendly driving patterns.

  • New Jersey's minimum liability requirement of 35/70/25 is the legal floor, not a safe target — since New Jersey already requires PIP coverage, review whether that limit alone would cover a serious injury claim.
  • With an estimated 14.9% of New Jersey drivers uninsured, carrying uninsured/underinsured motorist coverage above the state minimum is one of the highest-value add-ons you can buy.
