Minimum Coverage Requirements

Michigan requires drivers to carry minimum liability insurance of 50/100/10 — meaning $50,000 per person for bodily injury, $100,000 per accident for bodily injury, and $10,000 for property damage. Personal Injury Protection (PIP) is also required, covering medical expenses regardless of fault. These are the legal minimums; most insurance professionals recommend carrying significantly higher limits to protect personal assets in a serious accident. A common recommendation is at least 100/300/100, which provides substantially more protection without a proportionally large premium increase.

Average Car Insurance Costs in Michigan

Drivers in Michigan typically pay approximately $2,310 per year ($193/month) for car insurance, based on 2026 industry average data. Individual premiums vary significantly based on driving record, age, vehicle type, annual mileage, and selected coverage levels. Drivers with clean records may see rates well below the state average, while those with violations, claims, or high-risk profiles may pay substantially more.

State Regulations and Key Rules

Michigan overhauled its no-fault system in 2019. Drivers can now choose their PIP level: unlimited, $500K, $250K, $50K, or opt out if covered by qualifying health insurance. Historically among the most expensive states for car insurance.

What Makes Michigan Unique

Michigan's 2019 no-fault reform was one of the most significant changes to state car insurance law in decades. The reform was intended to lower premiums by allowing drivers to select lower PIP limits, but high uninsured rates remain a challenge.

Uninsured Drivers in Michigan

Approximately 25.5% of drivers in Michigan are estimated to be uninsured. This is a notably high uninsured rate — UM/UIM coverage is strongly recommended and, in many cases, essential for adequate financial protection. Uninsured motorist (UM) coverage pays for your medical expenses and vehicle damage if you are hit by a driver without insurance or with insufficient coverage. Underinsured motorist (UIM) coverage fills the gap when the at-fault driver's policy limits are too low to cover your losses.

Risk Factors Specific to Michigan

Detroit's uninsured driver rate is among the highest in the nation, making UM coverage essential. Michigan winters produce significant ice and snow hazards statewide. Lake Michigan coastal areas receive heavy lake-effect snow that creates dangerous driving conditions.

What Michigan’s Minimum Actually Covers

Michigan’s minimum is 50/100/10: $50,000 per injured person, $100,000 total per accident for injuries, and $10,000 for property damage. With new vehicles averaging roughly $50,000, the $10,000 property damage limit would leave about $40,000 unpaid if you totaled an average new car — and that gap becomes your personal debt. On the injury side, a single hospital admission after a serious crash can exceed $50,000 quickly; anything above the limit can be pursued from your assets and future wages.

No-Fault, PIP, and Required Add-Ons in Michigan

Michigan is a no-fault state: after most accidents, your own personal injury protection (PIP) pays your medical bills and some lost wages regardless of who caused the crash. Required PIP: a choice of levels, from $50,000 (Medicaid enrollees) up to unlimited. You can generally sue the at-fault driver for pain and suffering only when injuries pass the state’s threshold. Uninsured motorist coverage isn’t mandatory in Michigan, though insurers generally must offer it and you may have to reject it in writing.

How Michigan Compares: Uninsured Drivers and Price

An estimated 25.5% of Michigan drivers are uninsured — roughly 1 in 4 — one of the highest uninsured rates in the country. At that rate, uninsured motorist coverage is one of the most valuable add-ons you can buy.

Our average premium estimate of $2,310 a year puts Michigan among the ten most expensive states in our 50-state estimates (median $1,395). Your own price depends far more on your record, age, vehicle, and ZIP code than on the statewide average.

How to Save on Car Insurance in Michigan

Detroit has some of the highest car insurance rates of any major U.S. city — drivers there should aggressively compare quotes and consider the 2019 reform's PIP options carefully. Drivers with strong health insurance may benefit from selecting lower PIP limits to reduce premiums. Uninsured motorist coverage is critical given Michigan's high uninsured driver rate.

  • Compare quotes from at least 3–5 insurers annually — rates for identical coverage can vary by hundreds of dollars.
  • Michigan's minimum liability requirement of 50/100/10 is the legal floor, not a safe target — since Michigan already requires PIP coverage, review whether that limit alone would cover a serious injury claim. Most agents recommend at least 100/300/100 for meaningful asset protection.
  • Ask about bundling discounts if you also need home or renters insurance.
  • With an estimated 25.5% of Michigan drivers uninsured, carrying uninsured/underinsured motorist coverage above the state minimum is one of the highest-value add-ons you can buy.
  • Review your coverage annually, especially after major life changes: moving, buying a new vehicle, or adding a teen driver.
