Minimum Coverage Requirements

Oklahoma requires drivers to carry minimum liability insurance of 25/50/25 — meaning $25,000 per person for bodily injury, $50,000 per accident for bodily injury, and $25,000 for property damage.

State Regulations and Key Rules

Oklahoma is an at-fault state. Uninsured motorist coverage must be offered but can be rejected. Oklahoma is part of Tornado Alley, and severe weather events including hail, tornadoes, and flooding significantly impact comprehensive claims.

What Makes Oklahoma Unique

Oklahoma sees some of the highest rates of severe weather-related vehicle damage in the U.S. Comprehensive coverage is strongly recommended, particularly in central and western parts of the state.

Uninsured Drivers in Oklahoma

Approximately 13.4% of drivers in Oklahoma are estimated to be uninsured.

Risk Factors Specific to Oklahoma

Oklahoma sits in one of the most tornado-prone regions in the world — comprehensive coverage is non-negotiable. Hail damage is extremely common, particularly in the western and central parts of the state. Ice storms in winter create dangerous road conditions, particularly on the elevated Turner Turnpike.

What Oklahoma’s Minimum Actually Covers

Oklahoma’s minimum is 25/50/25: $25,000 per injured person, $50,000 total per accident for injuries, and $25,000 for property damage. With new vehicles averaging roughly $50,000, the $25,000 property damage limit would leave about $25,000 unpaid if you totaled an average new car — and that gap becomes your personal debt.

No-Fault, PIP, and Required Add-Ons in Oklahoma

Uninsured motorist coverage isn’t mandatory in Oklahoma, though insurers generally must offer it and you may have to reject it in writing.

How Oklahoma Compares: Uninsured Drivers and Price

An estimated 13.4% of Oklahoma drivers are uninsured — roughly 1 in 7 — a middle-of-the-pack uninsured rate. At that rate, uninsured motorist coverage is one of the most valuable add-ons you can buy.

Our average premium estimate of $1,640 a year puts Oklahoma above the national middle in our 50-state estimates (median $1,395).

How to Save on Car Insurance in Oklahoma

Comprehensive coverage is essential in Oklahoma — but choosing a higher deductible ($1,000 or more) can keep premiums manageable while still protecting against tornado and hail damage. Oklahoma City and Tulsa have the state's highest rates. Telematics programs suit drivers with moderate daily commutes.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the minimum car insurance in Oklahoma?

25/50/25: $25,000 of bodily injury liability per person, $50,000 per accident, and $25,000 of property damage liability.

Is Oklahoma a no-fault state?

No. Oklahoma is an at-fault state, so the driver who causes an accident is responsible for the resulting injuries and damage, usually through their liability insurance.

How many drivers are uninsured in Oklahoma?

An estimated 13.4% of Oklahoma drivers are uninsured, roughly 1 in 7.
