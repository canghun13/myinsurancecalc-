State Regulations and Key Rules

New Hampshire is the only state that does not require car insurance. However, drivers must demonstrate financial responsibility if involved in an accident. Most drivers choose to carry insurance to protect against liability. If insurance is carried, minimums are 25/50/25.

What Makes New Hampshire Unique

New Hampshire's opt-out system is unique in the U.S. While insurance is not mandatory, uninsured drivers remain personally liable for damages. The state has strict financial responsibility laws that effectively make insurance the practical choice for most drivers.

Uninsured Drivers in New Hampshire

Approximately 6.1% of drivers in New Hampshire are estimated to be uninsured. This is among the lower uninsured rates in the country, though carrying uninsured motorist coverage remains a sound financial decision.

Risk Factors Specific to New Hampshire

New Hampshire winters are severe — heavy snow, ice storms, and black ice are significant hazards from November through March. The White Mountains region presents particular challenges for mountain driving. Moose collisions are among the most dangerous wildlife incidents in the state.

What New Hampshire’s Minimum Actually Covers

New Hampshire is the only state that doesn’t require most drivers to buy auto insurance; instead, you must be able to show financial responsibility after an accident. If you do buy a policy, it must include at least 25/50/25 liability, $1,000 of medical payments coverage, and uninsured motorist coverage. Driving uninsured means paying for any damage or injuries you cause out of pocket, and losing your license and registration until you do.

No-Fault, PIP, and Required Add-Ons in New Hampshire

New Hampshire also requires UM/UIM coverage, which pays when the at-fault driver has no insurance or too little. Medical payments coverage of at least $1,000 is also part of the required package.

How New Hampshire Compares: Uninsured Drivers and Price

How to Save on Car Insurance in New Hampshire

New Hampshire's low rates and low uninsured driver rate reflect a responsible driving culture. Even though insurance is not legally required, voluntarily carrying at least 50/100/50 liability provides essential financial protection. Bundling home and auto with regional New England carriers often yields competitive rates.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is car insurance required in New Hampshire?

Most New Hampshire drivers are not required to buy auto insurance, but they must be able to show financial responsibility after an accident. Policies that are purchased must include at least 25/50/25 liability, $1,000 of medical payments coverage, and uninsured motorist coverage.

Is uninsured motorist coverage required in New Hampshire?

Yes. New Hampshire requires UM/UIM coverage.

How many drivers are uninsured in New Hampshire?

An estimated 6.1% of New Hampshire drivers are uninsured, roughly 1 in 16.
