Workers Compensation Requirements in Alaska

Alaska requires workers' compensation for virtually all employers with one or more employees. Alaska has some of the highest workers' comp rates in the nation, driven by the hazardous nature of many Alaskan industries (fishing, oil and gas, construction, logging) and high medical costs. Coverage is obtained through private insurers or qualified self-insurance.

Workers Comp Rates in Alaska

The average rate of approximately $2.84 per $100 of payroll is a statewide blended average — actual rates vary significantly by job classification.

For example, an employer with $1 million in payroll at the average rate of $2.84 per $100 would pay approximately $28,400 annually before experience modification adjustments.

What Makes Alaska Unique

Alaska's hazardous industry mix — commercial fishing, oil field work, construction in remote locations, and logging — produces among the highest workers' compensation claim rates and costs in the nation. Commercial fishing in Alaska is particularly high-risk, with fatal injury rates far exceeding national averages.

Who Sets Workers’ Comp Rates in Alaska

Alaska is an NCCI state. NCCI files advisory loss costs and class codes, and each insurer applies its own approved multiplier, plus schedule credits or debits, to arrive at your rate. That’s why quotes on identical payroll and class codes can differ meaningfully in Alaska, and why comparing carriers at renewal is worth the effort. Our blended estimate of about $2.84 per $100 of payroll hides an enormous range — clerical work is typically a small fraction of that, roofing many times more.

Alaska’s Maximum Weekly Benefit

Injured workers in Alaska receive wage-loss benefits based on their average weekly wage, up to a statutory cap. The maximum weekly benefit is $1,627.00, effective January 1, 2026 (Social Security Administration chart of state maximums). The cap matters for employers with well-paid staff: a claim by a high earner is paid at the maximum, so lost-time claims from skilled trades and professionals cost more than claims from lower-wage roles — one reason the experience modifier responds so strongly to a single serious claim.

Workers Comp Benefits in Alaska

Alaska provides wage replacement at 80% of the worker's spendable weekly wage (net of taxes), which is unique among states. Medical benefits cover all necessary treatment. Alaska's wage replacement rate is among the more generous in the nation.

Employee Rights in Alaska

Alaska workers can choose their own treating physician after the first 10 days. Disputes are handled by the Alaska Workers' Compensation Board. Alaska workers have the right to be represented by an attorney in disputed claims.

How to Reduce Workers Comp Costs in Alaska

Alaska employers in high-hazard industries should invest heavily in safety programs — the high base rates mean safety improvements have outsized financial impact. Experience modification management is critical. Employers in fishing and oil field work should work with insurers experienced in those specific industries.

Frequently Asked Questions

Does shopping lower workers’ comp premiums in Alaska?

Usually, yes. Alaska is an NCCI loss cost state, so each insurer applies its own multiplier and schedule credits to the same loss costs, and quotes on identical payroll can differ.

What is the maximum weekly workers’ comp benefit in Alaska?

$1,627.00, effective January 1, 2026.
