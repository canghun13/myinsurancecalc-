Workers Compensation Requirements in Illinois

Illinois requires workers' compensation for virtually all employers with one or more employees. Illinois has one of the more complex and litigious workers' comp systems in the nation. The Illinois Workers' Compensation Commission (IWCC) oversees the system. Illinois is known for higher claim costs due to generous benefits and significant attorney involvement.

Workers Comp Rates in Illinois

The average rate of approximately $2.38 per $100 of payroll is a statewide blended average — actual rates vary significantly by job classification.

For example, an employer with $1 million in payroll at the average rate of $2.38 per $100 would pay approximately $23,800 annually before experience modification adjustments.

What Makes Illinois Unique

Illinois workers' comp is notable for its high level of attorney involvement and litigation compared to most states. The combination of liberal benefits, Chicago's large plaintiff attorney market, and a system that encourages contested claims results in higher system costs. Illinois employers consistently face some of the highest workers' comp rates in the Midwest.

Who Sets Workers’ Comp Rates in Illinois

Illinois is an NCCI state. NCCI files advisory loss costs and class codes, and each insurer applies its own approved multiplier, plus schedule credits or debits, to arrive at your rate. That’s why quotes on identical payroll and class codes can differ meaningfully in Illinois, and why comparing carriers at renewal is worth the effort. Our blended estimate of about $2.38 per $100 of payroll hides an enormous range — clerical work is typically a small fraction of that, roofing many times more.

Illinois’s Maximum Weekly Benefit

Injured workers in Illinois receive wage-loss benefits based on their average weekly wage, up to a statutory cap. The maximum weekly benefit is $2,008.60, effective January 15, 2026 (Social Security Administration chart of state maximums). The cap matters for employers with well-paid staff: a claim by a high earner is paid at the maximum, so lost-time claims from skilled trades and professionals cost more than claims from lower-wage roles — one reason the experience modifier responds so strongly to a single serious claim.

Workers Comp Benefits in Illinois

Illinois provides temporary total disability at 66.67% of average weekly wage. Permanent partial disability uses a wage differential approach or scheduled awards. Illinois does not cap permanent total disability benefits.

Employee Rights in Illinois

Illinois workers can choose their own physician. The IWCC provides arbitrators and commissioners for dispute resolution. Illinois has significant worker advocacy through the attorney system.

How to Reduce Workers Comp Costs in Illinois

Illinois employers should focus on early claim reporting, medical management, and proactive return-to-work programs to minimize litigation risk. Working with experienced adjusters familiar with the Illinois system is critical. Chicago-area employers face particularly high litigation rates and should invest in robust safety and claims management programs.

Frequently Asked Questions

Does shopping lower workers’ comp premiums in Illinois?

Usually, yes. Illinois is an NCCI loss cost state, so each insurer applies its own multiplier and schedule credits to the same loss costs, and quotes on identical payroll can differ.

What is the maximum weekly workers’ comp benefit in Illinois?

$2,008.60, effective January 15, 2026.
