Workers Compensation Requirements in Pennsylvania

Pennsylvania requires workers' compensation for all employers with one or more employees. Pennsylvania has a state fund (State Workers' Insurance Fund — SWIF) that serves as the insurer of last resort and competes to some extent with private carriers. The Workers' Compensation Office of Adjudication handles disputes. Pennsylvania has a relatively employer-friendly system compared to neighboring New York and New Jersey.

Workers Comp Rates in Pennsylvania

The average rate of approximately $2.02 per $100 of payroll is a statewide blended average — actual rates vary significantly by job classification.

For example, an employer with $1 million in payroll at the average rate of $2.02 per $100 would pay approximately $20,200 annually before experience modification adjustments.

What Makes Pennsylvania Unique

Pennsylvania's SWIF provides coverage to employers who cannot obtain private market coverage, particularly smaller or higher-risk businesses. Pennsylvania allows employers to direct medical care for the first 90 days through a panel of physicians — one of the longer employer-directed periods among states — which helps control medical costs.

Who Sets Workers’ Comp Rates in Pennsylvania

Pennsylvania doesn’t use NCCI for ratemaking. Rates and classifications come from the Pennsylvania Compensation Rating Bureau (PCRB), an independent rating bureau, and Pennsylvania’s class codes and rules can differ from those used in NCCI states. Employers operating in several states should expect a Pennsylvania class code that doesn’t match the code on their other policies, and should check it at every audit.

Where The State Workers’ Insurance Fund (SWIF) Fits in the Pennsylvania Market

The State Workers’ Insurance Fund (SWIF) is run by the Department of Labor & Industry as the insurer of last resort for employers who can’t get coverage elsewhere. If private carriers decline you, start there.

Pennsylvania’s Maximum Weekly Benefit

Injured workers in Pennsylvania receive wage-loss benefits based on their average weekly wage, up to a statutory cap. The maximum weekly benefit is $1,394.00, effective January 1, 2026 (Social Security Administration chart of state maximums). The cap matters for employers with well-paid staff: a claim by a high earner is paid at the maximum, so lost-time claims from skilled trades and professionals cost more than claims from lower-wage roles — one reason the experience modifier responds so strongly to a single serious claim.

Workers Comp Benefits in Pennsylvania

Pennsylvania provides temporary total disability at 66.67% of average weekly wage. Employers can direct medical care for 90 days through a posted panel of physicians. Permanent disability benefits use a work loss approach and impairment ratings.

Employee Rights in Pennsylvania

Pennsylvania workers must use the employer's panel physicians for the first 90 days but can then choose any provider. Workers' Compensation Judges handle formal hearings.

How to Reduce Workers Comp Costs in Pennsylvania

Pennsylvania employers should build a strong physician panel — quality occupational medicine providers reduce claim duration. The 90-day employer-directed care period is a significant cost control tool that should be fully utilized. SWIF provides coverage for employers struggling with private market availability. Safety programs targeting manufacturing, construction, and healthcare (major Pennsylvania industries) reduce claim frequency.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is workers' comp insurance required in Pennsylvania?

Yes. Pennsylvania requires workers' compensation coverage for employers with 1 or more employees. Employers can also buy coverage through the State Workers' Insurance Fund (SWIF), which serves as an insurer of last resort and competes with private carriers.

How much does workers' comp cost in Pennsylvania?

The statewide blended average in Pennsylvania is approximately $2.02 per $100 of payroll, though actual rates vary significantly by job classification and claims history — high-risk classifications like construction and roofing pay substantially more than clerical or retail classifications.

Who regulates workers' comp in Pennsylvania?

Pennsylvania's Bureau of Workers' Compensation oversees Pennsylvania's workers' compensation system, handling claims administration, compliance, and dispute resolution between employees, employers, and insurers.

What happens if a Pennsylvania employer doesn't carry required workers' comp coverage?

Operating without required coverage exposes a Pennsylvania employer to civil penalties, potential personal liability for injury costs, and loss of the exclusive remedy protection that normally shields compliant employers from direct lawsuits over workplace injuries.

Does Pennsylvania use NCCI for workers’ comp?

No. Pennsylvania rates and classifications come from the Pennsylvania Compensation Rating Bureau (PCRB), an independent rating bureau.

What is the maximum weekly workers’ comp benefit in Pennsylvania?

$1,394.00, effective January 1, 2026.

What is State Workers’ Insurance Fund (SWIF)?

The State Workers’ Insurance Fund (SWIF) is run by the Department of Labor & Industry as the insurer of last resort for employers who can't get coverage elsewhere.
