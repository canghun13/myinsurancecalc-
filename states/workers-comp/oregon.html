Workers Compensation Requirements in Oregon

Oregon requires workers' compensation for all employers with one or more employees. Oregon has a state fund (SAIF Corporation) that competes with private insurers. The Workers' Compensation Division within the Department of Consumer and Business Services oversees the system. Oregon has a relatively comprehensive workers' comp system with strong worker protections.

Workers Comp Rates in Oregon

The average rate of approximately $1.88 per $100 of payroll is a statewide blended average — actual rates vary significantly by job classification.

For example, an employer with $1 million in payroll at the average rate of $1.88 per $100 would pay approximately $18,800 annually before experience modification adjustments.

What Makes Oregon Unique

SAIF Corporation (Oregon's competitive state fund) has one of the largest market shares among state funds in competitive markets. SAIF is known for its safety consulting services and proactive loss prevention approach. Oregon's workers' comp system includes strong vocational rehabilitation requirements.

Who Sets Workers’ Comp Rates in Oregon

Oregon is an NCCI state. NCCI files advisory loss costs and class codes, and each insurer applies its own approved multiplier, plus schedule credits or debits, to arrive at your rate. That’s why quotes on identical payroll and class codes can differ meaningfully in Oregon, and why comparing carriers at renewal is worth the effort. Our blended estimate of about $1.88 per $100 of payroll hides an enormous range — clerical work is typically a small fraction of that, roofing many times more.

Where SAIF Corporation Fits in the Oregon Market

SAIF Corporation is Oregon’s state-chartered not-for-profit insurer and the largest writer in the state; it also serves as the insurer of last resort. If private carriers decline you, start there.

Workers Comp Benefits in Oregon

Oregon provides temporary total disability at 66.67% of average weekly wage. Oregon has a strong vocational rehabilitation program for workers who cannot return to their previous employment.

Employee Rights in Oregon

Oregon workers may choose their own treating physician. The Workers' Compensation Board provides administrative law judges for formal hearings with access to the Court of Appeals.

How to Reduce Workers Comp Costs in Oregon

Oregon employers should compare SAIF with private insurer options — both typically offer competitive pricing. SAIF's loss control services add value beyond just insurance. Oregon's strong vocational rehabilitation requirements make return-to-work program planning important. Safety investments in logging, construction, and manufacturing produce measurable premium reductions.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is workers' comp insurance required in Oregon?

Yes. Oregon requires workers' compensation coverage for employers with 1 or more employees. SAIF Corporation, Oregon's state-chartered fund, competes with private insurers rather than holding a monopoly.

How much does workers' comp cost in Oregon?

The statewide blended average in Oregon is approximately $1.88 per $100 of payroll, though actual rates vary significantly by job classification and claims history — high-risk classifications like construction and roofing pay substantially more than clerical or retail classifications.

Who regulates workers' comp in Oregon?

Oregon's Workers' Compensation Division, part of the Department of Consumer and Business Services oversees Oregon's workers' compensation system, handling claims administration, compliance, and dispute resolution between employees, employers, and insurers.

What happens if a Oregon employer doesn't carry required workers' comp coverage?

Operating without required coverage exposes a Oregon employer to civil penalties, potential personal liability for injury costs, and loss of the exclusive remedy protection that normally shields compliant employers from direct lawsuits over workplace injuries.

Does shopping lower workers’ comp premiums in Oregon?

Usually, yes. Oregon is an NCCI loss cost state, so each insurer applies its own multiplier and schedule credits to the same loss costs, and quotes on identical payroll can differ.

What is SAIF Corporation?

SAIF Corporation is Oregon's state-chartered not-for-profit insurer and the largest writer in the state; it also serves as the insurer of last resort.
