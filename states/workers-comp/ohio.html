Workers Compensation Requirements in Ohio

Ohio is a monopolistic workers' comp state — all employers must purchase coverage from the Ohio Bureau of Workers' Compensation (BWC) state fund. Private insurance is not available. Ohio BWC is the largest state-operated workers' comp insurance program in the nation. Ohio allows large employers to self-insure with BWC approval.

Workers Comp Rates in Ohio

The average rate of approximately $1.72 per $100 of payroll is a statewide blended average — actual rates vary significantly by job classification.

For example, an employer with $1 million in payroll at the average rate of $1.72 per $100 would pay approximately $17,200 annually before experience modification adjustments.

What Makes Ohio Unique

Ohio BWC administers one of the most comprehensive state workers' comp programs in the nation. Ohio allows large employers meeting financial requirements to self-insure, providing an alternative to BWC coverage. Ohio has implemented numerous reform initiatives including group rating programs that allow employers in the same industry to pool risk and potentially reduce premiums.

Who Sets Workers’ Comp Rates in Ohio

Ohio is one of four states with a monopolistic state fund: unless an employer qualifies to self-insure, workers’ comp must be bought from the Ohio Bureau of Workers’ Compensation (BWC). There is no shopping between carriers — the fund sets the rates. Because state-fund policies don’t include employer’s liability coverage, Ohio employers usually add a stop-gap endorsement to their general liability policy, and employees working in other states need separate coverage there. See our North Dakota guide for how stop-gap works.

Ohio’s Maximum Weekly Benefit

Injured workers in Ohio receive wage-loss benefits based on their average weekly wage, up to a statutory cap. The maximum weekly benefit is $1,281.00, effective January 1, 2026 (Social Security Administration chart of state maximums). The cap matters for employers with well-paid staff: a claim by a high earner is paid at the maximum, so lost-time claims from skilled trades and professionals cost more than claims from lower-wage roles — one reason the experience modifier responds so strongly to a single serious claim.

Workers Comp Benefits in Ohio

Ohio BWC provides temporary total disability, medical benefits, permanent partial disability, and death benefits. Ohio's managed care organizations (MCOs) handle medical claims management. Ohio has an extensive vocational rehabilitation program.

Employee Rights in Ohio

Ohio workers receive benefits through BWC and the MCO system. Disputes are handled by Industrial Commission hearing officers with appeal to the Court of Common Pleas.

How to Reduce Workers Comp Costs in Ohio

Ohio employers should participate in group rating programs where available — these programs allow employers with good safety records to achieve significant premium reductions. Large employers should evaluate self-insurance eligibility with BWC. Safety investments have direct impact on experience modification and group rating eligibility.

  • Ohio operates a monopolistic state workers' comp fund, so pricing isn't competitive between private carriers here — your main lever for controlling cost is your experience modification rate (EMR), not shopping insurers.

Frequently Asked Questions

Can I buy workers’ comp from a private insurer in Ohio?

No. Ohio is a monopolistic state fund state; unless you qualify to self-insure, coverage comes from the Ohio Bureau of Workers' Compensation (BWC). Employers usually add stop-gap employer’s liability coverage to their general liability policy.

What is the maximum weekly workers’ comp benefit in Ohio?

$1,281.00, effective January 1, 2026.
