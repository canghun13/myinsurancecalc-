Workers Compensation Requirements in Mississippi

Mississippi requires workers' compensation for employers with 5 or more employees. Mississippi's Workers' Compensation Commission oversees the system. Mississippi has a relatively streamlined system with lower claim costs than national averages, partially reflecting lower wage levels (which affect temporary disability benefit amounts).

Workers Comp Rates in Mississippi

The average rate of approximately $1.72 per $100 of payroll is a statewide blended average — actual rates vary significantly by job classification.

For example, an employer with $1 million in payroll at the average rate of $1.72 per $100 would pay approximately $17,200 annually before experience modification adjustments.

What Makes Mississippi Unique

Mississippi's 5-employee threshold is among the highest in the nation, creating significant liability risk for small businesses with 2-4 employees who are not required to carry coverage. Those employers face direct lawsuits from injured workers without the benefit of workers' comp immunity.

Who Sets Workers’ Comp Rates in Mississippi

Mississippi is an NCCI state. NCCI files advisory loss costs and class codes, and each insurer applies its own approved multiplier, plus schedule credits or debits, to arrive at your rate. That’s why quotes on identical payroll and class codes can differ meaningfully in Mississippi, and why comparing carriers at renewal is worth the effort. Our blended estimate of about $1.72 per $100 of payroll hides an enormous range — clerical work is typically a small fraction of that, roofing many times more.

When Coverage Becomes Mandatory in Mississippi

Mississippi requires coverage once an employer has five or more employees, including part-time workers. Owners crossing the threshold should arrange coverage before the hire that triggers it, not after — an injury in the gap is the employer’s to pay in full.

How Much Workers’ Comp Pays in Mississippi (2026)

For a worker who can’t work at all while recovering, Mississippi workers’ comp replaces part of lost wages with a weekly check, separate from medical bills, which are paid in full by the insurer with no deductible. The key numbers:

Benefit Mississippi
Weekly wage-loss benefit (temporary total disability)66⅔% of your average weekly wage
Maximum benefit$654.63 a week for injuries on or after January 1, 2026 (Social Security Administration chart of state maximums)
Waiting periodNo wage-loss benefit for the first 5 days of disability
Waiting period paid back?Yes — if you’re out more than about 14 days, the waiting days are paid retroactively

A worker averaging $900 a week before the injury would receive about $600 a week. Because the check is two-thirds of wages up to the maximum, anyone who earned more than about $982 a week receives the maximum instead.

Checks change when your situation does: if you return to light duty at lower pay, temporary total disability usually switches to a partial benefit based on the wage difference, and once a doctor finds you’ve reached maximum medical improvement, any permanent impairment is paid under a separate permanent-disability schedule. The Mississippi Workers’ Compensation Commission adjusts the maximum periodically and resolves disputes over the weekly rate. If you’re an employer, the premium side of Mississippi workers’ comp is covered above; for how a lost-time claim moves your premium, see our EMR calculator.

Owner and Officer Payroll Limits in Mississippi

When owners are covered by a Mississippi workers’ comp policy, insurers don’t simply use what the owner takes home. Rating rules set a minimum and maximum annual payroll for executive officers, and a set amount or cap for sole proprietors and partners, and the premium audit applies them. For example, an officer paid $40,000 is still counted at $49,400, and one paid $332,800 is capped at $166,400 — so at a rate of $3.00 per $100 of payroll, the officer adds between $1,482 and $4,992 a year to the premium.

Employee Rights in Mississippi

Mississippi workers select their own treating physician from a list provided by the employer. Disputes are handled by the Workers' Compensation Commission through administrative law judges.

How to Reduce Workers Comp Costs in Mississippi

Mississippi employers with 2-4 employees should strongly consider voluntary coverage — the liability exposure from an uninsured workplace injury could be devastating. Safety training and proper classification reduce premium costs. Gulf Coast employers in construction and maritime industries face higher rates and benefit most from safety investments.

Frequently Asked Questions

Does shopping lower workers’ comp premiums in Mississippi?

Usually, yes. Mississippi is an NCCI loss cost state, so each insurer applies its own multiplier and schedule credits to the same loss costs, and quotes on identical payroll can differ.

When is workers’ comp required in Mississippi?

Mississippi requires coverage once an employer has five or more employees, including part-time workers.

How much does workers’ comp pay in Mississippi?

Mississippi workers' comp pays 66⅔% of your average weekly wage for temporary total disability, up to $654.63 a week for injuries on or after January 1, 2026. There is a waiting period of 5 days, paid back if you are out more than about 14 days. Medical treatment for the injury is covered in full.

What is the minimum payroll for an owner on a workers’ comp policy in Mississippi?

For policies effective on or after March 1, 2026, a covered corporate officer in Mississippi is counted at no less than $49,400 and no more than $166,400 a year. Sole proprietors and partners who elect coverage are counted at a flat $49,200.
