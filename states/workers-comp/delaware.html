Workers Compensation Requirements in Delaware

Delaware requires workers' compensation for all employers with one or more employees. Coverage is obtained through private insurers or self-insurance. Delaware's Industrial Accident Board handles workers' comp disputes. Delaware has a relatively small workers' comp market given its small population.

Workers Comp Rates in Delaware

The average rate of approximately $1.78 per $100 of payroll is a statewide blended average — actual rates vary significantly by job classification.

For example, an employer with $1 million in payroll at the average rate of $1.78 per $100 would pay approximately $17,800 annually before experience modification adjustments.

What Makes Delaware Unique

Delaware's workers' comp system is overseen by the Industrial Accident Board, one of the older workers' comp administrative bodies in the nation. Delaware requires employers to post workers' comp information in the workplace and notify employees of their rights.

Who Sets Workers’ Comp Rates in Delaware

Delaware doesn’t use NCCI for ratemaking. Rates and classifications come from the Delaware Compensation Rating Bureau (DCRB), an independent rating bureau, and Delaware’s class codes and rules can differ from those used in NCCI states. Employers operating in several states should expect a Delaware class code that doesn’t match the code on their other policies, and should check it at every audit.

Delaware’s Maximum Weekly Benefit

Injured workers in Delaware receive wage-loss benefits based on their average weekly wage, up to a statutory cap. The maximum weekly benefit is $962.72, effective July 1, 2026 (Social Security Administration chart of state maximums). The cap matters for employers with well-paid staff: a claim by a high earner is paid at the maximum, so lost-time claims from skilled trades and professionals cost more than claims from lower-wage roles — one reason the experience modifier responds so strongly to a single serious claim.

Workers Comp Benefits in Delaware

Delaware provides temporary total disability at 66.67% of average weekly wage. Permanent disability benefits follow a schedule for specific body parts.

Employee Rights in Delaware

Delaware workers may choose their own physician. The Industrial Accident Board provides dispute resolution through hearings with a structured appeal process.

How to Reduce Workers Comp Costs in Delaware

Delaware's small market means fewer insurer options — working with a broker who specializes in Delaware workers' comp is helpful. Safety programs and return-to-work policies reduce costs. Proper worker classification is important to ensure correct premium calculation.

Frequently Asked Questions

Does Delaware use NCCI for workers’ comp?

No. Delaware rates and classifications come from the Delaware Compensation Rating Bureau (DCRB), an independent rating bureau.

What is the maximum weekly workers’ comp benefit in Delaware?

$962.72, effective July 1, 2026.
