Workers Compensation Requirements in California

California requires workers' compensation for all employers with one or more employees and has one of the largest workers' comp systems in the nation. California has a state fund (State Compensation Insurance Fund — SCIF) that is the largest workers' comp insurer in the state. California's workers' comp system is complex and heavily regulated.

Workers Comp Rates in California

The average rate of approximately $2.45 per $100 of payroll is a statewide blended average — actual rates vary significantly by job classification.

For example, an employer with $1 million in payroll at the average rate of $2.45 per $100 would pay approximately $24,500 annually before experience modification adjustments.

What Makes California Unique

California's workers' comp system is the largest in the United States and has been subject to significant reform efforts over the decades. California's system includes unique features such as the ability to file liens by medical providers, complex utilization review requirements, and significant litigation risk. California also requires coverage for household employees who work more than 52 hours in 90 days.

California is also phasing in a requirement that licensed contractors carry workers' compensation regardless of whether they have employees. Five classifications are already covered and the universal mandate is currently scheduled for January 1, 2028 — not 2026, as many sources still state. How Much Workers’ Comp Pays in California (2026)

For a worker who can’t work at all while recovering, California workers’ comp replaces part of lost wages with a weekly check, separate from medical bills, which are paid in full by the insurer with no deductible. The key numbers:

Benefit California
Weekly wage-loss benefit (temporary total disability)66⅔% of your average weekly wage
Maximum benefit$1,764.11 a week for injuries on or after January 1, 2026 (Social Security Administration chart of state maximums)
Waiting periodNo wage-loss benefit for the first 3 days of disability
Waiting period paid back?Yes — if you’re out more than about 14 days, the waiting days are paid retroactively

A worker averaging $900 a week before the injury would receive about $600 a week. Because the check is two-thirds of wages up to the maximum, anyone who earned more than about $2,646 a week receives the maximum instead.

Checks change when your situation does: if you return to light duty at lower pay, temporary total disability usually switches to a partial benefit based on the wage difference, and once a doctor finds you’ve reached maximum medical improvement, any permanent impairment is paid under a separate permanent-disability schedule. The Division of Workers’ Compensation (DWC) adjusts the maximum periodically and resolves disputes over the weekly rate. If you’re an employer, the premium side of California workers’ comp is covered above; for how a lost-time claim moves your premium, see our EMR calculator.

Owner and Officer Payroll Limits in California

When owners are covered by a California workers’ comp policy, insurers don’t simply use what the owner takes home. Rating rules set a minimum and maximum annual payroll for executive officers, and a set amount or cap for sole proprietors and partners, and the premium audit applies them. For example, an officer paid $40,000 is still counted at $63,700, and one paid $330,200 is capped at $165,100 — so at a rate of $3.00 per $100 of payroll, the officer adds between $1,911 and $4,953 a year to the premium.

Employee Rights in California

California workers can choose their own physician if they have pre-designated in writing before the injury, otherwise the employer directs care for the first 30 days. California has a strong system of workers' rights and significant attorney involvement in disputed claims.

How to Reduce Workers Comp Costs in California

California employers should work with experienced workers' comp brokers given the system's complexity. Strong medical management, utilization review, and early return-to-work programs are essential. SCIF provides a competitive alternative for employers struggling to find private market coverage. Proper job classification is critical in California's complex classification system.

Frequently Asked Questions

How much does workers’ comp pay in California?

California workers' comp pays 66⅔% of your average weekly wage for temporary total disability, up to $1,764.11 a week for injuries on or after January 1, 2026. There is a waiting period of 3 days, paid back if you are out more than about 14 days. Medical treatment for the injury is covered in full.

What is the minimum payroll for an owner on a workers’ comp policy in California?

For policies effective on or after September 1, 2025, a covered corporate officer in California is counted at no less than $63,700 and no more than $165,100 a year. Sole proprietors and partners who elect coverage are counted at actual earnings up to $165,100.
