Workers Compensation Requirements in North Carolina

North Carolina requires workers' compensation for employers with 3 or more employees. The North Carolina Industrial Commission oversees the system. North Carolina has a relatively efficient workers' comp system with moderate claim costs. North Carolina's manufacturing, construction, and agriculture sectors generate significant workers' comp activity.

Workers Comp Rates in North Carolina

The average rate of approximately $1.58 per $100 of payroll is a statewide blended average — actual rates vary significantly by job classification.

For example, an employer with $1 million in payroll at the average rate of $1.58 per $100 would pay approximately $15,800 annually before experience modification adjustments.

What Makes North Carolina Unique

North Carolina's Industrial Commission is one of the more efficient administrative bodies for workers' comp dispute resolution in the Southeast. North Carolina's manufacturing sector — particularly furniture, textiles, and increasingly aerospace and automotive — generates a significant share of workers' comp claims.

Who Sets Workers’ Comp Rates in North Carolina

North Carolina doesn’t use NCCI for ratemaking. Rates and classifications come from the North Carolina Rate Bureau (NCRB), an independent rating bureau, and North Carolina’s class codes and rules can differ from those used in NCCI states. Employers operating in several states should expect a North Carolina class code that doesn’t match the code on their other policies, and should check it at every audit.

When Coverage Becomes Mandatory in North Carolina

North Carolina requires coverage at three or more employees, with stricter rules for certain industries such as businesses using radiation and some construction subcontracting. Owners crossing the threshold should arrange coverage before the hire that triggers it, not after — an injury in the gap is the employer’s to pay in full.

How Much Workers’ Comp Pays in North Carolina (2026)

For a worker who can’t work at all while recovering, North Carolina workers’ comp replaces part of lost wages with a weekly check, separate from medical bills, which are paid in full by the insurer with no deductible. The key numbers:

Benefit North Carolina
Weekly wage-loss benefit (temporary total disability)66⅔% of your average weekly wage
Maximum benefitSet each year by the North Carolina Industrial Commission, tied to the statewide average wage
Waiting periodNo wage-loss benefit for the first 7 days of disability
Waiting period paid back?Yes — if you’re out more than about 21 days, the waiting days are paid retroactively

A worker averaging $900 a week before the injury would receive about $600 a week, subject to the state maximum.

Checks change when your situation does: if you return to light duty at lower pay, temporary total disability usually switches to a partial benefit based on the wage difference, and once a doctor finds you’ve reached maximum medical improvement, any permanent impairment is paid under a separate permanent-disability schedule. The North Carolina Industrial Commission adjusts the maximum periodically and resolves disputes over the weekly rate. Owner and Officer Payroll Limits in North Carolina

When owners are covered by a North Carolina workers’ comp policy, insurers don’t simply use what the owner takes home. Rating rules set a minimum and maximum annual payroll for executive officers, and a set amount or cap for sole proprietors and partners, and the premium audit applies them. For example, an officer paid $40,000 is still counted at $65,000, and one paid $260,000 is capped at $130,000 — so at a rate of $3.00 per $100 of payroll, the officer adds between $1,950 and $3,900 a year to the premium.

Employee Rights in North Carolina

North Carolina workers may choose their own treating physician. Disputes are resolved through Deputy Commissioners at the Industrial Commission with a structured appeals process.

How to Reduce Workers Comp Costs in North Carolina

North Carolina's competitive market and efficient system reward safety investments with meaningful premium reductions. Manufacturing employers should focus on machine guarding, ergonomics, and lockout/tagout as top safety priorities. Experience modification management through strong return-to-work programs is highly effective.

Frequently Asked Questions

Does North Carolina use NCCI for workers’ comp?

No. North Carolina rates and classifications come from the North Carolina Rate Bureau (NCRB), an independent rating bureau.

When is workers’ comp required in North Carolina?

North Carolina requires coverage at three or more employees, with stricter rules for certain industries such as businesses using radiation and some construction subcontracting.

How much does workers’ comp pay in North Carolina?

North Carolina workers' comp pays 66⅔% of your average weekly wage for temporary total disability. There is a waiting period of 7 days, paid back if you are out more than about 21 days. Medical treatment for the injury is covered in full.

What is the minimum payroll for an owner on a workers’ comp policy in North Carolina?

For policies effective on or after April 1, 2026, a covered corporate officer in North Carolina is counted at no less than $65,000 and no more than $130,000 a year. Sole proprietors and partners who elect coverage are counted at a flat $65,300.
