Workers Compensation Requirements in Nebraska

Nebraska requires workers' compensation for virtually all employers. Nebraska's Workers' Compensation Court oversees the system — Nebraska is one of a few states with a dedicated workers' comp court. Nebraska has a relatively efficient system with moderate claim costs.

Workers Comp Rates in Nebraska

The average rate of approximately $1.55 per $100 of payroll is a statewide blended average — actual rates vary significantly by job classification.

For example, an employer with $1 million in payroll at the average rate of $1.55 per $100 would pay approximately $15,500 annually before experience modification adjustments.

What Makes Nebraska Unique

Nebraska uses a Workers' Compensation Court — a specialized judicial body — rather than an administrative agency for dispute resolution. This court structure provides more formal legal proceedings but also more predictability and established precedent in how cases are decided.

Who Sets Workers’ Comp Rates in Nebraska

Nebraska is an NCCI state. NCCI files advisory loss costs and class codes, and each insurer applies its own approved multiplier, plus schedule credits or debits, to arrive at your rate. That’s why quotes on identical payroll and class codes can differ meaningfully in Nebraska, and why comparing carriers at renewal is worth the effort. Our blended estimate of about $1.55 per $100 of payroll hides an enormous range — clerical work is typically a small fraction of that, roofing many times more.

Nebraska’s Maximum Weekly Benefit

Injured workers in Nebraska receive wage-loss benefits based on their average weekly wage, up to a statutory cap. The maximum weekly benefit is $1,166.00, effective January 1, 2026 (Social Security Administration chart of state maximums). The cap matters for employers with well-paid staff: a claim by a high earner is paid at the maximum, so lost-time claims from skilled trades and professionals cost more than claims from lower-wage roles — one reason the experience modifier responds so strongly to a single serious claim.

Workers Comp Benefits in Nebraska

Nebraska provides temporary total disability at 66.67% of average weekly wage. Permanent partial disability uses a schedule for specific body parts.

Employee Rights in Nebraska

Nebraska workers may choose their own physician. The Workers' Compensation Court provides formal judicial proceedings for disputed claims with a structured appeals process.

How to Reduce Workers Comp Costs in Nebraska

Nebraska's court-based dispute resolution system makes early claim resolution particularly valuable — avoiding formal court proceedings saves time and cost. Agricultural employers should consult an expert on coverage requirements for farm labor. Safety investments in manufacturing and construction produce meaningful experience modification improvements.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is workers' comp insurance required in Nebraska?

Yes. Nebraska requires workers' compensation coverage for employers with 1 or more employees. Nebraska allows employers to self-insure with state approval, and coverage can be purchased from private insurers with no state-run fund option.

How much does workers' comp cost in Nebraska?

The statewide blended average is approximately $1.55 per $100 of payroll, though actual rates vary significantly by job classification and claims history — high-risk classifications like construction and roofing pay substantially more than clerical or retail classifications.

Who regulates workers' comp in Nebraska?

The Nebraska Workers' Compensation Court oversees Nebraska's workers' compensation system, handling claims administration, compliance, and dispute resolution between employees, employers, and insurers.

What happens if a Nebraska employer doesn't carry required workers' comp coverage?

Operating without required coverage exposes an employer to civil penalties, potential personal liability for injury costs, and loss of the exclusive remedy protection that normally shields compliant employers from direct lawsuits over workplace injuries.

Does shopping lower workers’ comp premiums in Nebraska?

Usually, yes. Nebraska is an NCCI loss cost state, so each insurer applies its own multiplier and schedule credits to the same loss costs, and quotes on identical payroll can differ.

What is the maximum weekly workers’ comp benefit in Nebraska?

$1,166.00, effective January 1, 2026.
