Workers Compensation Requirements in Florida

Florida requires workers' compensation for employers with 4 or more employees (non-construction) and 1 or more employees in construction. Florida has a large and competitive workers' comp market. The state has undergone significant reform over the past two decades aimed at controlling costs. Florida's Division of Workers' Compensation within the Department of Financial Services oversees the system.

Workers Comp Rates in Florida

The average rate of approximately $1.95 per $100 of payroll is a statewide blended average — actual rates vary significantly by job classification.

For example, an employer with $1 million in payroll at the average rate of $1.95 per $100 would pay approximately $19,500 annually before experience modification adjustments.

What Makes Florida Unique

Florida's construction industry has the most stringent workers' comp requirements of any industry — even sole proprietor contractors must have coverage or obtain an exemption certificate. This reflects the high injury rate in construction. Florida contractors and subcontractors must verify coverage of all subcontractors or face liability for their workers.

Who Sets Workers’ Comp Rates in Florida

Florida is an NCCI state. NCCI files advisory loss costs and class codes, and each insurer applies its own approved multiplier, plus schedule credits or debits, to arrive at your rate. That’s why quotes on identical payroll and class codes can differ meaningfully in Florida, and why comparing carriers at renewal is worth the effort. Our blended estimate of about $1.95 per $100 of payroll hides an enormous range — clerical work is typically a small fraction of that, roofing many times more.

When Coverage Becomes Mandatory in Florida

Florida requires coverage at four or more employees outside construction and from the first employee in construction, counting corporate officers and LLC members unless exempt. Owners crossing the threshold should arrange coverage before the hire that triggers it, not after — an injury in the gap is the employer’s to pay in full.

Florida’s Maximum Weekly Benefit

Injured workers in Florida receive wage-loss benefits based on their average weekly wage, up to a statutory cap. The maximum weekly benefit is $1,358.00, effective January 1, 2026 (Social Security Administration chart of state maximums). The cap matters for employers with well-paid staff: a claim by a high earner is paid at the maximum, so lost-time claims from skilled trades and professionals cost more than claims from lower-wage roles — one reason the experience modifier responds so strongly to a single serious claim.

Workers Comp Benefits in Florida

Florida provides temporary total disability at 66.67% of average weekly wage, capped at state maximums. Florida has a managed care option that many large employers use to control medical costs.

Employee Rights in Florida

Florida uses an employer-directed medical care model — the employer or insurer controls the initial treating physician selection. Disputes are handled by judges of compensation claims.

How to Reduce Workers Comp Costs in Florida

Florida construction employers must ensure all subcontractors have valid workers' comp or obtain exemptions — failure to do so creates direct liability. Medical managed care programs can significantly reduce claim costs. Return-to-work programs in Florida's competitive labor market help retain experienced workers.

Frequently Asked Questions

Does shopping lower workers’ comp premiums in Florida?

Usually, yes. Florida is an NCCI loss cost state, so each insurer applies its own multiplier and schedule credits to the same loss costs, and quotes on identical payroll can differ.

$1,358.00, effective January 1, 2026.
