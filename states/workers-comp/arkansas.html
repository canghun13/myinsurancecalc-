Workers Compensation Requirements in Arkansas

Arkansas requires workers' compensation for employers with 3 or more employees. Agricultural employers are generally exempt. Coverage is obtained through private insurers or qualified self-insurance. Arkansas Workers' Compensation Commission oversees the system.

Workers Comp Rates in Arkansas

The average rate of approximately $1.68 per $100 of payroll is a statewide blended average — actual rates vary significantly by job classification.

For example, an employer with $1 million in payroll at the average rate of $1.68 per $100 would pay approximately $16,800 annually before experience modification adjustments.

What Makes Arkansas Unique

Arkansas's 3-employee threshold exempts very small businesses from mandatory coverage, but creates liability risk for those employers. Arkansas's agricultural exemption is significant given the state's rural economy — farm workers typically need separate coverage or are not covered.

Who Sets Workers’ Comp Rates in Arkansas

Arkansas is an NCCI state. NCCI files advisory loss costs and class codes, and each insurer applies its own approved multiplier, plus schedule credits or debits, to arrive at your rate. That’s why quotes on identical payroll and class codes can differ meaningfully in Arkansas, and why comparing carriers at renewal is worth the effort. Our blended estimate of about $1.68 per $100 of payroll hides an enormous range — clerical work is typically a small fraction of that, roofing many times more.

When Coverage Becomes Mandatory in Arkansas

Arkansas requires coverage once an employer has three or more employees; construction subcontracting arrangements follow stricter rules. Owners crossing the threshold should arrange coverage before the hire that triggers it, not after — an injury in the gap is the employer’s to pay in full.

Arkansas’s Maximum Weekly Benefit

Injured workers in Arkansas receive wage-loss benefits based on their average weekly wage, up to a statutory cap. The maximum weekly benefit is $953.00, effective January 1, 2026 (Social Security Administration chart of state maximums). The cap matters for employers with well-paid staff: a claim by a high earner is paid at the maximum, so lost-time claims from skilled trades and professionals cost more than claims from lower-wage roles — one reason the experience modifier responds so strongly to a single serious claim.

Workers Comp Benefits in Arkansas

Arkansas provides wage replacement at 66.67% of average weekly wage, subject to state maximums. Permanent disability benefits follow a scheduled award system.

Employee Rights in Arkansas

Arkansas workers select their own physician from an approved panel provided by the employer. Disputes are handled by the Arkansas Workers' Compensation Commission.

How to Reduce Workers Comp Costs in Arkansas

Arkansas employers with fewer than 3 employees should still consider voluntary coverage given direct liability exposure. Safety training and proper job classification reduce premium costs. Return-to-work programs reduce the duration and cost of claims.

Frequently Asked Questions

Usually, yes. Arkansas is an NCCI loss cost state, so each insurer applies its own multiplier and schedule credits to the same loss costs, and quotes on identical payroll can differ.
