Workers Compensation Requirements in New York

New York requires workers' compensation for all employers with one or more employees. New York has a state fund (New York State Insurance Fund — NYSIF) that is the largest workers' comp insurer in the state and competes with private carriers. New York also requires Disability Benefits Law (DBL) coverage for non-work-related disabilities. The Workers' Compensation Board oversees the system.

Workers Comp Rates in New York

The average rate of approximately $2.88 per $100 of payroll is a statewide blended average — actual rates vary significantly by job classification.

For example, an employer with $1 million in payroll at the average rate of $2.88 per $100 would pay approximately $28,800 annually before experience modification adjustments.

What Makes New York Unique

New York's NYSIF is one of the largest state insurance funds in the nation, insuring a very large share of New York workers. New York's workers' comp system is one of the more complex and expensive in the nation, with significant litigation, high medical costs, and above-average benefit levels. New York's DBL requirement for non-work-related disabilities adds to the total employer cost.

Who Sets Workers’ Comp Rates in New York

New York doesn’t use NCCI for ratemaking. Rates and classifications come from the New York Compensation Insurance Rating Board (NYCIRB), an independent rating bureau, and New York’s class codes and rules can differ from those used in NCCI states. Employers operating in several states should expect a New York class code that doesn’t match the code on their other policies, and should check it at every audit.

Where The New York State Insurance Fund (NYSIF) Fits in the New York Market

The New York State Insurance Fund (NYSIF) is the largest writer in New York and the insurer of last resort. If private carriers decline you, start there.

New York’s Maximum Weekly Benefit

Injured workers in New York receive wage-loss benefits based on their average weekly wage, up to a statutory cap. The maximum weekly benefit is $1,222.42, effective July 1, 2025 (Social Security Administration chart of state maximums). The cap matters for employers with well-paid staff: a claim by a high earner is paid at the maximum, so lost-time claims from skilled trades and professionals cost more than claims from lower-wage roles — one reason the experience modifier responds so strongly to a single serious claim.

Workers Comp Benefits in New York

New York provides temporary total disability at 66.67% of average weekly wage. DBL covers non-work disabilities at 50% of wages. New York has a schedule for permanent partial disability covering various body parts.

Employee Rights in New York

New York workers may choose their own physician. The Workers' Compensation Board provides law judges for formal hearings and appeals panels for review.

How to Reduce Workers Comp Costs in New York

New York City employers face some of the highest workers' comp rates in the nation — safety programs and experience modification management are essential. NYSIF provides competitive pricing especially for smaller employers. DBL must be managed alongside workers' comp. Return-to-work programs are especially valuable in New York's high-wage, high-benefit environment.
