Workers Compensation Requirements in Connecticut

Connecticut requires workers' compensation for all employers with one or more employees. Connecticut does not have a state fund; coverage must be obtained from private insurers or through self-insurance. Connecticut's Workers' Compensation Commission handles disputes and system oversight.

Workers Comp Rates in Connecticut

The average rate of approximately $2.12 per $100 of payroll is a statewide blended average — actual rates vary significantly by job classification.

For example, an employer with $1 million in payroll at the average rate of $2.12 per $100 would pay approximately $21,200 annually before experience modification adjustments.

What Makes Connecticut Unique

Connecticut's workers' comp system has relatively high medical costs compared to national averages, driven by the high cost of healthcare in the state. Connecticut also has a Second Injury Fund that protects employers from excess liability when a worker with a pre-existing disability suffers a new injury — this fund helps employers willing to hire workers with disabilities.

Who Sets Workers’ Comp Rates in Connecticut

Connecticut is an NCCI state. NCCI files advisory loss costs and class codes, and each insurer applies its own approved multiplier, plus schedule credits or debits, to arrive at your rate. That’s why quotes on identical payroll and class codes can differ meaningfully in Connecticut, and why comparing carriers at renewal is worth the effort. Our blended estimate of about $2.12 per $100 of payroll hides an enormous range — clerical work is typically a small fraction of that, roofing many times more.

Connecticut’s Maximum Weekly Benefit

Injured workers in Connecticut receive wage-loss benefits based on their average weekly wage, up to a statutory cap. The maximum weekly benefit is $1,716.00, effective October 1, 2025 (Social Security Administration chart of state maximums). The cap matters for employers with well-paid staff: a claim by a high earner is paid at the maximum, so lost-time claims from skilled trades and professionals cost more than claims from lower-wage roles — one reason the experience modifier responds so strongly to a single serious claim.

Workers Comp Benefits in Connecticut

Connecticut provides temporary total disability at 75% of after-tax (net) average weekly wage — one of the more generous replacement rates. Connecticut's maximum weekly benefit is relatively high.

Employee Rights in Connecticut

Connecticut workers have broad rights to select their own physician and receive comprehensive benefits. The Workers' Compensation Commission provides dispute resolution through formal hearings with commissioner decisions.

How to Reduce Workers Comp Costs in Connecticut

Connecticut employers face above-average workers' comp costs — safety investments have significant payback. Return-to-work programs are particularly valuable in Connecticut's high-wage environment where temporary disability costs accumulate quickly. Proper job classification review can identify savings opportunities.

Frequently Asked Questions

Does shopping lower workers’ comp premiums in Connecticut?

Usually, yes. Connecticut is an NCCI loss cost state, so each insurer applies its own multiplier and schedule credits to the same loss costs, and quotes on identical payroll can differ.

What is the maximum weekly workers’ comp benefit in Connecticut?

$1,716.00, effective October 1, 2025.
