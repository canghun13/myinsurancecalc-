Workers Compensation Requirements in West Virginia

West Virginia privatized its workers' comp system in 2005, transitioning from a monopolistic state fund to a competitive private market. BrickStreet Insurance (now Encova Insurance) emerged from the former state fund. The Workers' Compensation Office of Judges handles disputes. West Virginia's mining, construction, and chemical manufacturing industries generate significant workers' comp claims.

Workers Comp Rates in West Virginia

The average rate of approximately $2.12 per $100 of payroll is a statewide blended average — actual rates vary significantly by job classification.

For example, an employer with $1 million in payroll at the average rate of $2.12 per $100 would pay approximately $21,200 annually before experience modification adjustments.

What Makes West Virginia Unique

West Virginia's transition from a monopolistic state fund to a competitive private market in 2005 was one of the most significant workers' comp system transformations of that decade. The transition was necessitated by massive unfunded liabilities in the old state fund. West Virginia's privatization is studied as a model of how to exit a failing monopolistic system.

Who Sets Workers’ Comp Rates in West Virginia

West Virginia is an NCCI state. NCCI files advisory loss costs and class codes, and each insurer applies its own approved multiplier, plus schedule credits or debits, to arrive at your rate. That’s why quotes on identical payroll and class codes can differ meaningfully in West Virginia, and why comparing carriers at renewal is worth the effort. Our blended estimate of about $2.12 per $100 of payroll hides an enormous range — clerical work is typically a small fraction of that, roofing many times more.

Where Encova Insurance Fits in the West Virginia Market

Encova Insurance is descended from BrickStreet, the private insurer created when West Virginia ended its monopolistic state fund; the private market fully opened in 2008. It competes with private carriers, so get its quote alongside at least one other insurer rather than treating either as the default.

Workers Comp Benefits in West Virginia

West Virginia provides temporary total disability at 70% of average weekly wage. Permanent partial disability uses an impairment rating approach.

Employee Rights in West Virginia

West Virginia workers may choose their own treating physician. The Workers' Compensation Office of Judges handles formal hearings with appeal to the Workers' Compensation Board of Review.

How to Reduce Workers Comp Costs in West Virginia

West Virginia employers in coal mining and chemical manufacturing face above-average premiums — specialized safety programs are essential. The 2005 privatization created a competitive market — shopping insurers is now possible. Experience modification management through safety investment and return-to-work programs is key to long-term premium reduction.
