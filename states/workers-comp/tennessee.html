Workers Compensation Requirements in Tennessee

Tennessee requires workers' compensation for employers with 5 or more employees (1 or more in construction). Tennessee's Bureau of Workers' Compensation within the Department of Labor and Workforce Development oversees the system. Tennessee underwent significant workers' comp reform in 2014 creating an administrative court system.

Workers Comp Rates in Tennessee

Workers compensation premiums in Tennessee are calculated based on payroll, job classification (NCCI or state classification codes), and the employer's experience modification factor. The average rate of approximately $1.68 per $100 of payroll is a statewide blended average — actual rates vary significantly by job classification. A clerical office worker might pay $0.20-$0.40 per $100, while a roofing contractor might pay $15-$25 per $100.

For example, an employer with $1 million in payroll at the average rate of $1.68 per $100 would pay approximately $16,800 annually before experience modification adjustments. Employers with better-than-average loss experience (experience mod below 1.0) pay less; those with worse experience pay more.

What Makes Tennessee Unique

Tennessee's 2014 reform created the Court of Workers' Compensation Claims — a specialized court system — replacing the prior general civil court approach. This reform significantly reduced litigation costs and improved the efficiency of dispute resolution. Tennessee's rates have moderated following the reform.

Tennessee Has Two Thresholds, Not One

Most states apply a single coverage trigger. Tennessee applies two, and which one governs you depends on your industry.

  • Most employers: coverage is required at five or more employees.
  • Construction services providers: coverage is required from the first employee, under the construction-specific provisions of Tennessee Code §50-6-901 and following. The statute is explicit that this applies whether or not the provider employs fewer than five people.

The five-employee count is broader than it looks. Part-time, seasonal, minor, and working family members all count toward it. And a detail that catches corporations specifically: a corporate officer who exempts himself still counts as an employee for the threshold. A company with four workers and one exempt officer has five employees and needs coverage.

The Construction Exemption Registry Covers the Owner, Never the Crew

Tennessee lets eligible construction business owners exempt themselves through the Workers' Compensation Exemption Registry, administered by the Bureau of Workers' Compensation. To qualify, the applicant must have an ownership stake: a sole proprietor owning 100% of the business, a partner with at least a 20% interest, or an applicant who together with family members holds at least 95% of the business.

The rule that matters most is stated plainly by the state: exempt owners must still carry coverage for every employee, even if they have only one. The registry is an owner exemption, not a business exemption. A roofer on the registry who hires a single helper for a weekend job needs a policy for that helper.

The registry is public and searchable, which is how general contractors verify a sub's claimed exemption. Being listed also says nothing about licensing status. On commercial projects, a registry-listed sub is only exempt from the coverage requirement when the party they are working for complies with the statute's own verification duties — see how to verify subcontractor insurance for why the hiring contractor carries the risk when that goes wrong.

What Tennessee Workers' Comp Actually Costs

Tennessee is one of the cheaper states to insure in, with average rates reported around $0.98 per $100 of payroll against a national average near $1.03. It is a competitive-rating state, so carriers file their own multipliers and shopping genuinely moves the number.

Workers Comp Benefits in Tennessee

Tennessee provides temporary total disability at 66.67% of average weekly wage. Medical benefits cover necessary treatment. Permanent partial disability uses a whole body impairment approach for most injuries.

Employee Rights in Tennessee

Tennessee workers select their own treating physician from a panel of physicians provided by the employer. Workers' Compensation Judges in the Court of Workers' Compensation Claims hear disputed claims.

How Workers Comp Premiums Are Calculated in Tennessee

Workers compensation premiums in Tennessee follow this basic formula: (Payroll / 100) × Classification Rate × Experience Modification Factor = Premium. The experience modification factor (EMR or X-Mod) compares your actual loss experience to the expected loss experience for your industry. An EMR of 1.0 is average; below 1.0 means better-than-average safety record (lower premiums); above 1.0 means worse-than-average (higher premiums).

Job classification codes are assigned based on the type of work employees perform. Accurate classification is critical — misclassifying employees into lower-risk categories can result in premium audits and significant back-premium assessments. An employer with employees in multiple classifications must track payroll by class code throughout the year.

How to Reduce Workers Comp Costs in Tennessee

Tennessee construction employers must carry coverage from the first employee. Nashville's booming economy means increased workers' comp exposure for construction, healthcare, and hospitality employers. Tennessee's post-2014 reform environment is more predictable — safety investments and return-to-work programs have clearer impact on experience modification.

  • Implement a formal workplace safety program — documented safety training reduces injury frequency and improves experience modification over time.
  • Develop a return-to-work program — getting injured workers back on modified duty reduces temporary disability claim duration and cost.
  • Report all injuries promptly — early reporting enables timely medical treatment and claims management, reducing claim severity.
  • Review job classifications annually — ensure employees are classified in the correct (not simply the lowest-cost) classification code.
  • Since Tennessee workers' comp is written by competing carriers, shop your policy at each renewal — rates for identical classification codes and payroll can vary meaningfully between insurers.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is workers' comp insurance required in Tennessee?

Yes. Tennessee requires workers' compensation coverage for employers with 5 or more employees (construction industry: 1 or more). Tennessee singles out the construction industry with a stricter 1-employee threshold, reflecting the higher injury risk in that sector compared to the standard 5-employee threshold for other businesses.

How much does workers' comp cost in Tennessee?

The statewide blended average is approximately $1.68 per $100 of payroll, though actual rates vary significantly by job classification and claims history — high-risk classifications like construction and roofing pay substantially more than clerical or retail classifications.

Who regulates workers' comp in Tennessee?

The Tennessee Bureau of Workers' Compensation oversees Tennessee's workers' compensation system, handling claims administration, compliance, and dispute resolution between employees, employers, and insurers.

What happens if a Tennessee employer doesn't carry required workers' comp coverage?

Operating without required coverage exposes an employer to civil penalties, potential personal liability for injury costs, and loss of the exclusive remedy protection that normally shields compliant employers from direct lawsuits over workplace injuries.

How many employees before workers' comp is required in Tennessee?

Five or more for most employers, but one or more for construction services providers under Tennessee Code section 50-6-901 and following. Part-time, seasonal, minor, and working family members all count toward the five. A corporate officer who has exempted himself still counts as an employee for the threshold, so four workers plus one exempt officer equals five and triggers the requirement.

Can a Tennessee construction business owner exempt themselves from workers' comp?

Yes, through the Workers' Compensation Exemption Registry, if they hold an ownership stake: a sole proprietor owning 100%, a partner with at least 20%, or an applicant and family members together holding at least 95%. The exemption covers the owner only. Exempt owners must still carry coverage for every employee, even if they have just one.

What is the workers' comp rate for a clerical worker in Tennessee?

Clerical and office classifications can run as low as about $0.15 per $100 of payroll, against a statewide average reported around $0.98 and a class-code range of roughly $0.07 to $15.99. For a clerical employee earning $40,000 that implies roughly $60 a year before experience modification, minimum premium, and state assessments. Tennessee is a competitive-rating state, so quotes differ between carriers.
