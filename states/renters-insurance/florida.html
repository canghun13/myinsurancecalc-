What Makes Florida Renters Insurance Unique

Florida has the second-highest average renters insurance cost in the country, driven almost entirely by hurricane and named-storm exposure along the coasts.

Risk Factors Specific to Florida

Underwriters in Florida weigh hurricane, tropical storm, and wind damage more heavily than in lower-risk states when pricing renters policies.

Renters Insurance Compared With Rent in Florida

The median gross rent in Florida was $1,812 a month in 2024 (U.S. Census Bureau, American Community Survey), 22% above the national median of $1,487. Against that, our estimated renters premium of $245 a year — about $20 a month — equals roughly 14% of one month’s rent for a full year of coverage, above the middle share among the 50 states in our estimates.

The Coverage Detail That Matters Most in Florida

Renters in hurricane-exposed parts of Florida should know two things. First, renters policies exclude flood, including storm surge — contents flood coverage is a separate purchase, available through the National Flood Insurance Program for up to $100,000 of belongings. Second, some policies in coastal areas carry a separate named-storm deductible. Additional living expenses coverage pays for a hotel if a covered storm makes your unit unlivable, but not if you simply evacuate under orders with no damage, unless the policy includes civil authority coverage.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is renters insurance required in Florida?

No. No U.S. state, including Florida, legally requires tenants to carry renters insurance. That said, individual landlords and property management companies in Florida can — and often do — require proof of a policy as a lease condition, especially for larger apartment complexes.

How much does renters insurance cost in Florida?

Renters in Florida pay an estimated $245 per year ($20/month) for a standard policy, based on 2026 industry average data. Actual premiums vary based on coverage limits, deductible, credit history (where allowed), claims history, and the specific building or neighborhood.

Can insurers use your credit score to set renters insurance rates in Florida?

Yes. Florida allows insurers to factor in your credit-based insurance score when calculating renters insurance premiums, alongside coverage limits, claims history, and location. Renters with stronger credit typically qualify for lower rates from most carriers.

What's the biggest renters insurance risk factor in Florida?

In Florida, hurricane and windstorm damage is the top coverage concern. Standard renters policies do cover wind damage to personal property, but they typically exclude flood damage from storm surge — that requires a separate flood insurance policy, which is worth considering even as a renter in flood-prone areas.

How much is renters insurance compared with rent in Florida?

With a median rent of $1,812 a month (2024) and an estimated premium of $245 a year, a full year of renters insurance in Florida costs roughly 14% of one month's rent.

Does renters insurance cover flood or storm surge in Florida?

No. Renters policies exclude flood, including storm surge. Contents flood coverage is available separately through the National Flood Insurance Program, for up to $100,000 of belongings.
