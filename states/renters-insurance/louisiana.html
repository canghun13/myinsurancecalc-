What Makes Louisiana Renters Insurance Unique

Louisiana has the highest average renters insurance cost in the nation, a direct result of repeated hurricane, storm surge, and flooding claims along the Gulf Coast.

Risk Factors Specific to Louisiana

Underwriters in Louisiana weigh hurricane, tropical storm, and wind damage more heavily than in lower-risk states when pricing renters policies.

Renters Insurance Compared With Rent in Louisiana

The median gross rent in Louisiana was $1,064 a month in 2024 (U.S. Census Bureau, American Community Survey), 28% below the national median of $1,487. Against that, our estimated renters premium of $266 a year — about $22 a month — equals roughly 25% of one month’s rent for a full year of coverage, one of the ten highest share among the 50 states in our estimates.

The Coverage Detail That Matters Most in Louisiana

Renters in hurricane-exposed parts of Louisiana should know two things. First, renters policies exclude flood, including storm surge — contents flood coverage is a separate purchase, available through the National Flood Insurance Program for up to $100,000 of belongings. Second, some policies in coastal areas carry a separate named-storm deductible. Additional living expenses coverage pays for a hotel if a covered storm makes your unit unlivable, but not if you simply evacuate under orders with no damage, unless the policy includes civil authority coverage.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is renters insurance required in Louisiana?

No. No U.S. state, including Louisiana, legally requires tenants to carry renters insurance. That said, individual landlords and property management companies in Louisiana can — and often do — require proof of a policy as a lease condition, especially for larger apartment complexes.

How much does renters insurance cost in Louisiana?

Renters in Louisiana pay an estimated $266 per year ($22/month) for a standard policy, based on 2026 industry average data. Actual premiums vary based on coverage limits, deductible, credit history (where allowed), claims history, and the specific building or neighborhood.

Can insurers use your credit score to set renters insurance rates in Louisiana?

Yes. Louisiana allows insurers to factor in your credit-based insurance score when calculating renters insurance premiums, alongside coverage limits, claims history, and location. Renters with stronger credit typically qualify for lower rates from most carriers.

What's the biggest renters insurance risk factor in Louisiana?

In Louisiana, hurricane and windstorm damage is the top coverage concern. Standard renters policies do cover wind damage to personal property, but they typically exclude flood damage from storm surge — that requires a separate flood insurance policy, which is worth considering even as a renter in flood-prone areas.

How much is renters insurance compared with rent in Louisiana?

With a median rent of $1,064 a month (2024) and an estimated premium of $266 a year, a full year of renters insurance in Louisiana costs roughly 25% of one month's rent.

Does renters insurance cover flood or storm surge in Louisiana?

No. Renters policies exclude flood, including storm surge. Contents flood coverage is available separately through the National Flood Insurance Program, for up to $100,000 of belongings.
