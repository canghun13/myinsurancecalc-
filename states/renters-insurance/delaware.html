What Makes Delaware Renters Insurance Unique

Delaware has no state sales tax, which occasionally leads renters to underestimate replacement costs for electronics and furniture when setting coverage limits.

Risk Factors Specific to Delaware

Underwriters in Delaware weigh a mix of moderate weather and property-crime risk more heavily than in lower-risk states when pricing renters policies.

Renters Insurance Compared With Rent in Delaware

The median gross rent in Delaware was $1,530 a month in 2024 (U.S. Census Bureau, American Community Survey), 3% above the national median of $1,487. Against that, our estimated renters premium of $158 a year — about $13 a month — equals roughly 10% of one month’s rent for a full year of coverage, below the middle share among the 50 states in our estimates.

The Coverage Detail That Matters Most in Delaware

Delaware renters face a mix of risks rather than one dominant peril, so the details of the policy matter more than the headline price. Check whether personal property is covered at replacement cost or actual cash value: an actual-cash-value policy pays depreciated prices, which can mean a few hundred dollars for a laptop that costs over a thousand to replace.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is renters insurance required in Delaware?

No. No U.S. state, including Delaware, legally requires tenants to carry renters insurance. That said, individual landlords and property management companies in Delaware can — and often do — require proof of a policy as a lease condition, especially for larger apartment complexes.

How much does renters insurance cost in Delaware?

Renters in Delaware pay an estimated $158 per year ($13/month) for a standard policy, based on 2026 industry average data. Actual premiums vary based on coverage limits, deductible, credit history (where allowed), claims history, and the specific building or neighborhood.

Can insurers use your credit score to set renters insurance rates in Delaware?

Yes. Delaware allows insurers to factor in your credit-based insurance score when calculating renters insurance premiums, alongside coverage limits, claims history, and location. Renters with stronger credit typically qualify for lower rates from most carriers.

What's the biggest renters insurance risk factor in Delaware?

In Delaware, risk factors are relatively balanced across weather, theft, and liability exposure — no single peril dominates pricing here, which keeps rates closer to the national middle range.

How much is renters insurance compared with rent in Delaware?

With a median rent of $1,530 a month (2024) and an estimated premium of $158 a year, a full year of renters insurance in Delaware costs roughly 10% of one month's rent.
