What Makes California Renters Insurance Unique

California bans the use of credit scores in insurance pricing, and wildfire-adjacent ZIP codes in the foothills and wildland-urban interface can see noticeably higher quotes than coastal metro areas.

Risk Factors Specific to California

Underwriters in California weigh wildfire, smoke damage, and evacuation-related loss more heavily than in lower-risk states when pricing renters policies.

Renters Insurance Compared With Rent in California

The median gross rent in California was $2,104 a month in 2024 (U.S. Census Bureau, American Community Survey), 41% above the national median of $1,487. Against that, our estimated renters premium of $175 a year — about $15 a month — equals roughly 8% of one month’s rent for a full year of coverage, one of the ten lowest share among the 50 states in our estimates.

The Coverage Detail That Matters Most in California

In California, the wildfire question for renters is usually about displacement rather than the building itself. Additional living expenses coverage pays for temporary housing if a covered fire makes your unit unlivable, and many policies also pay for a limited period when a civil authority bars access. Smoke damage to belongings is generally covered as part of fire. Check the limit on additional living expenses — in high-rent areas, a short displacement can exhaust a low limit.

Earthquake Coverage for California Renters

Renters policies exclude earthquake damage. The California Earthquake Authority sells a renters earthquake policy through participating insurers that covers personal property and additional living expenses after a quake, with its own deductible. Because the cost is modest for renters, it’s worth pricing if you live near a major fault.

How to Save on Renters Insurance in California

  • California bans or restricts insurers from using your credit-based insurance score to price renters coverage, so your premium here is driven more by coverage limits and claims history than by your credit.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is renters insurance required in California?

No. No U.S. state, including California, legally requires tenants to carry renters insurance. That said, individual landlords and property management companies in California can — and often do — require proof of a policy as a lease condition, especially for larger apartment complexes.

How much does renters insurance cost in California?

Renters in California pay an estimated $175 per year ($15/month) for a standard policy, based on 2026 industry average data. Actual premiums vary based on coverage limits, deductible, credit history (where allowed), claims history, and the specific building or neighborhood.

Can insurers use your credit score to set renters insurance rates in California?

No. California is one of a handful of states that bans or significantly restricts the use of credit-based insurance scores when pricing renters insurance. Insurers must base your premium on other factors instead, such as coverage limits, claims history, and location.

What's the biggest renters insurance risk factor in California?

In California, wildfire is the top concern. Standard renters policies do cover fire damage, including wildfire, but tenants in high-risk fire zones should confirm their insurer is still writing new policies in that area and consider replacement cost coverage given rising rebuild costs.

How much is renters insurance compared with rent in California?

With a median rent of $2,104 a month (2024) and an estimated premium of $175 a year, a full year of renters insurance in California costs roughly 8% of one month's rent.
