What Makes Alaska Renters Insurance Unique

Alaska's low population density and low renters-insurance claim frequency keep base premiums among the lowest in the country, even though remoteness can raise the cost of replacing high-value electronics or gear.

Risk Factors Specific to Alaska

Underwriters in Alaska weigh high replacement costs for electronics and personal property more heavily than in lower-risk states when pricing renters policies.

Renters Insurance Compared With Rent in Alaska

The median gross rent in Alaska was $1,444 a month in 2024 (U.S. Census Bureau, American Community Survey), 3% below the national median of $1,487. Against that, our estimated renters premium of $101 a year — about $8 a month — equals roughly 7% of one month’s rent for a full year of coverage, one of the ten lowest share among the 50 states in our estimates.

The Coverage Detail That Matters Most in Alaska

In Alaska, replacing belongings costs more than in most states because goods have to be shipped in, so the coverage limit you need is higher than a mainland estimate suggests. Choose replacement cost coverage for personal property rather than actual cash value, and inventory your belongings at local prices before choosing a limit.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is renters insurance required in Alaska?

No. No U.S. state, including Alaska, legally requires tenants to carry renters insurance. That said, individual landlords and property management companies in Alaska can — and often do — require proof of a policy as a lease condition, especially for larger apartment complexes.

How much does renters insurance cost in Alaska?

Renters in Alaska pay an estimated $101 per year ($8/month) for a standard policy, based on 2026 industry average data. Actual premiums vary based on coverage limits, deductible, credit history (where allowed), claims history, and the specific building or neighborhood.

Can insurers use your credit score to set renters insurance rates in Alaska?

Yes. Alaska allows insurers to factor in your credit-based insurance score when calculating renters insurance premiums, alongside coverage limits, claims history, and location. Renters with stronger credit typically qualify for lower rates from most carriers.

What's the biggest renters insurance risk factor in Alaska?

In Alaska, the leading cost factor is replacement cost rather than any single peril. Shipping and logistics costs for replacing damaged or stolen belongings run higher here, so it's worth choosing a personal property limit that accounts for these higher effective replacement costs.

How much is renters insurance compared with rent in Alaska?

With a median rent of $1,444 a month (2024) and an estimated premium of $101 a year, a full year of renters insurance in Alaska costs roughly 7% of one month's rent.
