General Liability Insurance: Not Required, But Often Necessary

What General Liability Actually Costs in Vermont

Vermont sits close to the national midpoint for general liability pricing. Industry cost surveys for 2026 put a typical Vermont small business with one to four employees at roughly $122 per month, or about $1,464 a year, for a standard $1 million per occurrence / $2 million aggregate policy — essentially level with the national average of around $123 per month, and the second most affordable state in New England behind Maine.

Treat that as a baseline for a small, low-to-moderate risk operation rather than a quote. Industry classification is the single largest driver, followed by annual revenue, the limits your contracts require, and claims history. A Burlington professional services firm and a ski-country contractor working at height will not land anywhere near the same number on identical limits.

Because Vermont imposes no blanket general liability requirement, the practical minimum is whatever your counterparties demand. Commercial leases commonly require $1 million per occurrence; municipal, state and institutional contracts frequently require $1 million / $2 million with the entity named as an additional insured; and general contractors routinely require subcontractors to carry equal or higher limits before jobsite access. Buying once to the highest limit your contracts require is usually cheaper than endorsing a policy upward client by client.

Two structural features of Vermont are worth factoring into your shopping. First, the state's economy is heavily weighted toward seasonal tourism, hospitality and outdoor recreation — if your revenue is concentrated in a few peak months, ask specifically about business interruption coverage with a seasonal endorsement, because a standard calculation based on trailing-twelve-month average revenue will understate a peak-season closure. Second, Vermont has an unusually deep commercial insurance infrastructure for its size as a leading domicile for captive insurance; for most small businesses that is background noise, but it does mean local brokers are often more comfortable than average with non-standard and specialty placements.

What Makes Vermont Business Insurance Unique

Vermont requires workers' comp from the first employee, and its low population density and low commercial litigation rate keep small business insurance among the more affordable in New England.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is workers' comp insurance required in Vermont?

Yes. Vermont requires workers' compensation coverage once a business has 1 employee. Sole proprietors and owners without employees are generally exempt for themselves, though LLC managers and corporate officers may be included or excluded depending on their role.

How much does small business insurance cost in Vermont?

A standard Business Owner's Policy (BOP) bundling general liability and commercial property coverage costs an estimated $650 per year for a small business in Vermont, based on industry average data. Actual premiums vary by industry, payroll size, and claims history.

Is general liability insurance required in Vermont?

No, general liability insurance isn't legally mandated in Vermont for most businesses. It's frequently required contractually, though — commercial landlords, client contracts, and certain licensed professions often require proof of coverage.

Where do Vermont businesses buy workers' comp insurance?

Vermont businesses purchase workers' compensation coverage from private insurers.

How much is general liability insurance in Vermont?

A typical Vermont small business with one to four employees pays roughly $122 per month, or about $1,464 per year, for a $1 million per occurrence / $2 million aggregate general liability policy in 2026 — essentially level with the national average and the second most affordable rate in New England after Maine. Actual premiums vary widely by industry classification, revenue, required limits and claims history.

What general liability limits do Vermont businesses usually need?

Vermont does not require general liability insurance by statute for most businesses, so the effective minimum is set by contract. Commercial leases commonly require $1 million per occurrence, while municipal, state and institutional contracts frequently require $1 million per occurrence and $2 million aggregate with the entity named as an additional insured. General contractors typically require subcontractors to match or exceed their own limits.

What insurance is legally required for a small business in Vermont?

Workers' compensation (required at 1 employee), and commercial auto liability of at least 25/50/10 for business vehicles. General liability is not required by statute for most businesses but is commonly required by leases and contracts.
