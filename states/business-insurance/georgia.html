When Is Workers' Compensation Required in Georgia?

Georgia's workers' compensation requirement: 3 employees. Sole proprietors and business owners without employees are generally exempt from carrying it for themselves, though LLC members and corporate officers may be included or excluded depending on their management role and how many are involved. If you're unsure whether a specific worker triggers the requirement — especially part-time, seasonal, or 1099 contractor arrangements — it's worth confirming with Georgia's workers' compensation agency directly, since misclassifying an employee as a contractor can result in retroactive premiums and penalties.

Workers' Comp Insurance Market in Georgia

Georgia allows businesses to purchase workers' compensation coverage from private insurance carriers, so it's worth comparing quotes from multiple companies rather than accepting the first offer. Pricing depends on your industry classification code, total payroll, and claims history (reflected in your experience modification rate).

Estimated Small Business Insurance Cost in Georgia

A standard Business Owner's Policy (BOP) — which bundles general liability and commercial property coverage — costs an estimated $760 per year for a small business in Georgia, based on 2026 industry average data. Actual premiums vary significantly based on industry classification, payroll size, claims history, and specific coverage limits. Businesses with higher liability exposure (contractors, food service, retail with public foot traffic) typically pay more than low-risk professional services operating primarily online or by appointment.

General Liability Insurance: Not Required, But Often Necessary

Unlike workers' comp, general liability insurance isn't legally mandated in Georgia for most businesses. That said, it's frequently required contractually — commercial landlords typically require proof of general liability before signing a lease, many client contracts (especially government and enterprise clients) require it, and certain licensed professions in Georgia require it as a condition of maintaining their license. Even businesses with no legal obligation to carry it are exposed to third-party injury and property damage claims from day one of operation.

What Makes Georgia Business Insurance Unique

Georgia exempts businesses with fewer than 3 employees from mandatory workers' comp, and Atlanta's dense professional-services sector has made professional liability a common add-on for consultants and agencies.

LLC Owners and Workers' Comp in Georgia

If your business is structured as an LLC in Georgia, workers' comp treatment depends on whether members hold management authority. LLC managers who can sign contracts or make business decisions on the company's behalf are typically excluded from the requirement, while non-management members working for the LLC generally must be covered. This exemption is usually capped at a limited number of managers regardless of total membership, so larger LLCs should confirm their specific obligation rather than assuming full exemption.

Georgia Requires Workers’ Comp at Three Employees

Georgia’s State Board of Workers’ Compensation requires coverage once a business has three or more employees. That sits between states that require it from the first hire and states like Florida that use four for most businesses. For a small Georgia business, the third hire is the trigger — and since part-time employees regularly in service generally count toward the three, it often arrives before an owner expects it.

Once covered, the employer takes on obligations that have nothing to do with the premium: posting the physician panel, reporting injuries, and paying benefits on the statutory timeline. Those administrative duties are where Georgia employers most often lose control of claim costs.

The Panel of Physicians: Georgia’s Biggest Employer Cost Lever

Georgia lets employers direct an injured worker’s medical care — but only if they have posted a valid Panel of Physicians (Board form WC-P1) or contracted with a certified Workers’ Compensation Managed Care Organization. The panel has specific requirements:

  • At least six physicians (the Board can allow fewer where more than four are not reasonably accessible).
  • At least one orthopedic surgeon, and one minority physician whenever feasible.
  • No more than two industrial clinics.
  • Posted in a conspicuous place readily accessible to employees at all times.

The injured worker may make one change to another doctor on the panel without approval; further changes need the employer’s or the Board’s permission.

The practical risk is an invalid panel. A panel with doctors who have retired, moved, or stopped taking workers’ comp patients, or one that was never posted where employees could see it, can let the worker choose any physician at the employer’s expense — the employer loses the main tool it had for managing medical cost. Reviewing the panel once a year, and every time a listed practice changes, costs almost nothing.

The 21-day retroactive rule is worth knowing when budgeting a claim: a worker who returns on day 20 costs about two weeks of income benefits, while one who returns on day 22 costs three.

Georgia Workers’ Comp Rates for 2026

Georgia is an NCCI loss cost state: NCCI files advisory loss costs, and carriers apply their own approved multipliers. For 2026, NCCI proposed a −8.8% voluntary loss cost change and a −9.3% assigned risk rate change, effective March 1, 2026.

Note the effective date. Georgia’s rate year turns over in March, not January, so a Georgia policy renewing in January or February is still being priced on the prior year’s loss costs. Because carrier multipliers differ, Georgia is a state where comparing quotes on identical payroll and class codes can produce meaningfully different premiums.

What Georgia Doesn’t Require

It helps to know what is not on a Georgia employer’s list, because comparison guides written for New York, New Jersey, or California often imply otherwise:

  • No state disability insurance program. Georgia has no mandatory short-term disability or paid family leave insurance for private employers.
  • No no-fault auto system. Georgia is an at-fault state; business vehicles need liability coverage at or above the state minimums, and personal auto policies typically exclude or limit business use.
  • No statutory general liability requirement for most non-contractor businesses — though leases and client contracts usually impose one.

How to Lower Your Business Insurance Costs in Georgia

  • Bundle general liability and property coverage into a BOP — it's typically cheaper than buying each policy separately.
  • Classify workers correctly from the start — reclassification penalties and back premiums are far more expensive than getting it right the first time.
  • Shop your workers' comp classification code — a misclassified job code can meaningfully overstate your premium.
  • Maintain a documented safety program — many insurers offer credits for formal workplace safety and loss-prevention practices.
  • Since Georgia's workers' comp market is open to private insurers, shop that policy annually alongside your BOP — rates for identical coverage can vary significantly between carriers.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is workers' comp insurance required in Georgia?

Yes. Georgia requires workers' compensation coverage once a business has 3 employees. Sole proprietors and owners without employees are generally exempt for themselves, though LLC managers and corporate officers may be included or excluded depending on their role.

Is general liability insurance required in Georgia?

How many employees before workers’ comp is required in Georgia?

Three. Georgia requires workers’ compensation coverage once a business has three or more employees, and part-time employees regularly in service generally count toward that number.

What is a Georgia panel of physicians?

It is a posted list of at least six physicians, including at least one orthopedic surgeon and no more than two industrial clinics, from which an injured worker chooses a doctor. It must be posted where employees can always see it. The worker may make one change to another doctor on the panel without approval. Employers can alternatively use a certified workers’ compensation managed care organization.

What happens if a Georgia employer doesn’t post a valid physician panel?

The employer risks losing control over medical care: an invalid or unposted panel can allow the injured worker to choose any physician at the employer’s expense. Keeping the panel current and posted is one of the cheapest ways to control claim costs.

When do workers’ comp payments start in Georgia?

Weekly income benefits are not paid for the first seven days of disability. If the disability lasts more than 21 consecutive days, the first week is paid retroactively. Benefits are two-thirds of the average weekly wage up to the statutory maximum.

Are Georgia workers’ comp rates going down in 2026?

NCCI proposed an 8.8% decrease in Georgia voluntary market loss costs and a 9.3% decrease in assigned risk rates, effective March 1, 2026. Georgia’s rate year changes in March, so policies renewing earlier in the year use the prior loss costs.

Do Georgia contractors need general liability insurance?

Licensed contractors do. The State Licensing Board for Residential and General Contractors requires $300,000 per occurrence for Residential Basic and $500,000 per occurrence for Residential Light Commercial and Commercial General Contractor licenses, as a condition of applying for and maintaining the license.
