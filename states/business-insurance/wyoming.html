Wyoming Is a Monopolistic Workers' Comp State

Wyoming does not allow private insurance companies to sell workers' compensation coverage. Businesses must purchase their policy directly through the state's own workers' compensation fund rather than shopping between private carriers. This is a meaningful difference from most states, where you can compare quotes from multiple private insurers — in Wyoming, pricing and terms are set by the state program.

What Makes Wyoming Business Insurance Unique

Wyoming is a monopolistic workers' comp state — coverage must be purchased through the Wyoming Workers' Safety and Compensation program — and its no-income-tax, business-friendly formation laws make it a popular (though sometimes misunderstood) state for LLC registration.

Wyoming is one of four monopolistic states: workers’ comp comes only from the Wyoming Department of Workforce Services, Workers’ Compensation Division, so there are no competing quotes to compare — the savings levers are correct classification, safety, and the state fund’s own discount programs.

Our estimate of about $660 a year for a business owner’s policy in Wyoming covers general liability and business property for a low-risk small business; contractors, restaurants, and businesses with vehicles pay more, and the required coverages above are priced separately.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is workers' comp insurance required in Wyoming?

Yes. Wyoming requires workers' compensation coverage once a business has 1 employee. Sole proprietors and owners without employees are generally exempt for themselves, though LLC managers and corporate officers may be included or excluded depending on their role.

How much does small business insurance cost in Wyoming?

A standard Business Owner's Policy (BOP) bundling general liability and commercial property coverage costs an estimated $660 per year for a small business in Wyoming, based on industry average data. Actual premiums vary by industry, payroll size, and claims history.

Is general liability insurance required in Wyoming?

No, general liability insurance isn't legally mandated in Wyoming for most businesses. It's frequently required contractually, though — commercial landlords, client contracts, and certain licensed professions often require proof of coverage.

Where do Wyoming businesses buy workers' comp insurance?

Wyoming businesses purchase workers' compensation coverage from the state fund only (Wyoming is a monopolistic state — private workers' comp insurers aren't permitted).

What insurance is legally required for a small business in Wyoming?

Workers' compensation (required at 1 employee, bought from the state fund), and commercial auto liability of at least 25/50/20 for business vehicles. General liability is not required by statute for most businesses but is commonly required by leases and contracts.
