Health Insurance Overview in Nebraska

Nebraska expanded Medicaid in 2020 following voter approval through a 2018 ballot initiative — another example of voters acting where the legislature had not. Nebraska uses the federal HealthCare.gov marketplace. Nebraska has faced marketplace challenges in rural counties with limited insurer participation.

For 2027 coverage, the average benchmark plan — the second-lowest-cost silver plan — for a 40-year-old in Nebraska costs $780 a month before subsidies, up 10% from $710 in 2026. That makes Nebraska the 12th most expensive of the 50 states and DC; the national average is $703, up about 13%.

Three things decide what you actually pay. Income: below 400%% of the federal poverty level, the premium tax credit cuts the benchmark plan to a set share of income; above it, you pay these prices in full, because the temporary rules that capped premiums at 8.5%% of income ended after 2025. Metal level: bronze plans cost less, and in many areas the cheapest gold plan costs about the same as or less than benchmark silver (nationally $694 versus $703 in 2027), so compare before defaulting to silver. County: these are statewide averages, and your county’s benchmark can be well above or below them. Self-employed? Our self-employed health insurance calculator estimates the after-tax cost.

Medicaid in Nebraska

Nebraska Heritage Health Medicaid expanded in 2020, covering adults with incomes up to 138% of the federal poverty level following voter approval.

Children's Health Insurance (CHIP) in Nebraska

Nebraska CHIP covers children under 19 in families with incomes up to 200% of the federal poverty level.

What Makes Nebraska Unique

Nebraska's Medicaid expansion followed a similar path to Maine — voters approved expansion through a ballot initiative (2018) after the legislature repeatedly failed to act, and implementation began in 2020. Nebraska's expansion covered approximately 90,000 previously uninsured Nebraskans.

ACA Subsidies: What Nebraska Residents Should Know

The enhanced subsidies first enacted in the American Rescue Plan expired at the end of 2025. For 2026, premium tax credits follow the original ACA rules: there is no credit above 400% (the “subsidy cliff”), and enrollees are expected to pay up to 9.96% of income toward the benchmark Silver plan. House in January 2026 but has not become law, so 2027 plans are being priced and subsidized under the same rules unless Congress acts.

New for 2027: premium tax credits are limited to U.S. citizens, green card holders, Cuban/Haitian entrants and COFA migrants. Refugees, asylees, TPS holders and work or student visa holders can still enroll, but at full price — see 2027 ACA subsidies for immigrants.

Nebraska’s Marketplace for 2027: Insurers, Rates, and Dates

Nebraska residents enroll through HealthCare.gov. Full-price premiums rose about 29.1% on average for 2026, before subsidies. 5 insurers sold plans for 2026. Proposed 2027 changes average about 13.3% before subsidies.

About 128,000 people enrolled for 2026. About 87% received premium tax credits, and the average net premium after credits was about $217 a month.

Open enrollment for 2027: November 1, 2026 through January 15, 2027 on HealthCare.gov; enroll by December 15 for coverage starting January 1.

What’s Different About Health Coverage in Nebraska

Nebraska expanded Medicaid by ballot initiative, effective late 2020, covering adults up to 138% FPL. All five 2026 insurers — Blue Cross Blue Shield of Nebraska, Medica, Ambetter, Oscar, and UnitedHealthcare — return for 2027, with proposed increases averaging about 13%.

Tips for Choosing Health Insurance in Nebraska

Nebraska residents who were previously uninsured should check Medicaid eligibility following the 2020 expansion. Rural Nebraska residents may have limited marketplace options. ACA subsidies are available for incomes 100-400% of the federal poverty level. Nebraska CHIP covers children.

  • Enroll during open enrollment (November 1 – January 15 (federal marketplace)) — missing the deadline means waiting until the next open enrollment unless you have a qualifying life event.

Frequently Asked Questions

How many insurers sell marketplace plans in Nebraska?

When is open enrollment in Nebraska for 2027?

November 1, 2026 through January 15, 2027 on HealthCare.gov; enroll by December 15 for coverage starting January 1.

How much do Nebraska marketplace enrollees pay after subsidies?

In 2026 about 87% of Nebraska enrollees received premium tax credits, and the average net premium was about $217 a month. There is no credit above 400% of the federal poverty level.

How much does health insurance cost in Nebraska in 2027?

Before subsidies, the average benchmark silver plan in Nebraska costs $780 a month for a 40-year-old in 2027 (KFF). Using the federal age curve, that is roughly $610 at age 21, $1,656 at age 60 and $2,494 for a family of four with two young children. Households above 400% of the poverty level pay the full price; below that, the premium tax credit lowers it.
