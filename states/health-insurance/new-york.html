Health Insurance Overview in New York

New York operates NY State of Health, a state-based marketplace. New York expanded Medicaid well before the ACA and has one of the most comprehensive Medicaid programs in the nation. New York has a Basic Health Program (Essential Plan) for residents with incomes between 100% and 200% of the federal poverty level — covering nearly 1 million New Yorkers at minimal cost.

Medicaid in New York

New York Medicaid covers adults with incomes up to 138% of the federal poverty level, with comprehensive benefits. New York has expanded coverage beyond federal minimums significantly.

Children's Health Insurance (CHIP) in New York

Child Health Plus covers children up to 400% of the federal poverty level with comprehensive benefits.

What Makes New York Unique

New York's Essential Plan is a Basic Health Program that provides comprehensive coverage with very low premiums ($0-$20/month) for residents with incomes between 100-200% of the federal poverty level. The Essential Plan has enrolled over 1 million New Yorkers and is one of the most successful Basic Health Programs in the nation, serving as a model for other states.

ACA Subsidies: What New York Residents Should Know

The enhanced subsidies first enacted in the American Rescue Plan expired at the end of 2025. For 2026, premium tax credits follow the original ACA rules: there is no credit above 400% (the “subsidy cliff”), and enrollees are expected to pay up to 9.96% of income toward the benchmark Silver plan. The Essential Plan Changed on July 1, 2026

New York’s Essential Plan — the low-cost alternative to marketplace plans for lower-income adults — was reorganized mid-2026 because of federal rule changes. New York ended the Section 1332 waiver that had expanded the program and returned to running it as a Basic Health Program. The practical effects:

  • Income limit back to 200% FPL. The Essential Plan continues for adults up to 200% of the federal poverty level (about $31,920 a year for one person).
  • The 200–250% FPL group lost eligibility as of July 1, 2026 (roughly $31,920–$39,900 for one person). Those enrollees were moved toward Qualified Health Plans with premium tax credits and cost-sharing reductions.
  • DACA recipients above 138% FPL are no longer eligible for any NY State of Health program; some may still qualify for Medicaid or Child Health Plus.
  • Pregnant and postpartum enrollees with due dates before January 1, 2027 could remain in the Essential Plan through the postpartum period.

If you were moved from the Essential Plan to a Qualified Health Plan this year, open enrollment is the first chance to compare plans on equal terms. The plan you were defaulted into is not necessarily the best value at your income.

No Age Rating: Why New York Premiums Look Different

New York is one of very few states that prohibits age rating in the individual market. A plan costs the same premium for a 25-year-old and a 62-year-old in the same rating region; tobacco use doesn’t change it either. Most states allow insurers to charge older adults up to three times what they charge younger ones.

That cuts both ways. Young, healthy New Yorkers see full-price premiums well above what the same age pays in neighboring states, while people in their late 50s and early 60s pay less than they would elsewhere. Because tax credits are measured against the benchmark premium, an older New Yorker typically receives a smaller credit than a same-income person of the same age in an age-rated state — but the net premium is set by income either way, so the result is similar after subsidies.

Medicaid Changes in 2026 and 2027

  • July 1, 2026: adult Medicaid enrollees no longer receive 12 months of continuous coverage regardless of changes, and children under 6 returned to 12-month continuous eligibility.
  • January 1, 2027: adults in the expansion group must document 80 hours a month of work, community service, or half-time education, unless they qualify for an exemption. New York began mailing notices in July 2026.
  • From 2027: eligibility for expansion adults is rechecked every six months instead of once a year, under federal law.

If you are on Medicaid and your hours vary, keep pay stubs or school enrollment records each month — the documentation, not just the activity, is what keeps coverage in place.

Tips for Choosing Health Insurance in New York

New York residents should enroll through NY State of Health (not HealthCare.gov). The Essential Plan provides near-free coverage for incomes 100-200% of the federal poverty level. New York's extended open enrollment (through January 31) provides additional time. Child Health Plus provides comprehensive children's coverage. Medicaid covers comprehensive benefits for low-income adults.

  • Enroll during open enrollment (November 1 – January 31 (NY State of Health — extended deadline)) — missing the deadline means waiting until the next open enrollment unless you have a qualifying life event.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the Essential Plan income limit in New York now?

Since July 1, 2026 the Essential Plan covers adults up to 200% of the federal poverty level, about $31,920 a year for one person. People between 200% and 250% lost Essential Plan eligibility and were moved toward marketplace plans with premium tax credits.

Does age affect health insurance premiums in New York?

No. New York prohibits age rating in the individual market, so the same plan costs the same for a younger and an older adult in the same region, and tobacco use does not change the premium. Younger adults pay more than they would in most states; older adults pay less.

Do New York Medicaid recipients have to work starting in 2027?

Adults in the Medicaid expansion group must document 80 hours a month of work, community service, or half-time education starting January 1, 2027, unless they qualify for an exemption. Eligibility will also be rechecked every six months.

Can DACA recipients get health insurance through NY State of Health?

After the July 2026 changes, DACA recipients with income above 138% of the poverty level are no longer eligible for NY State of Health programs, including the Essential Plan. Some may still qualify for Medicaid or Child Health Plus.
