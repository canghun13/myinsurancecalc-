Health Insurance Overview in Pennsylvania

Pennsylvania operates Pennie, a state-based marketplace launched in 2021. Pennsylvania expanded Medicaid (Medicaid expansion / PA Healthy Communities) in 2015. Pennsylvania has a competitive marketplace in Philadelphia and Pittsburgh with multiple insurer options. The state has implemented consumer assistance programs to help residents navigate coverage options.

For 2027 coverage, the average benchmark plan — the second-lowest-cost silver plan — for a 40-year-old in Pennsylvania costs $648 a month before subsidies, up 13% from $572 in 2026. That makes Pennsylvania the 18th least expensive of the 50 states and DC; the national average is $703, up about 13%.

Three things decide what you actually pay. Income: below 400%% of the federal poverty level, the premium tax credit cuts the benchmark plan to a set share of income; above it, you pay these prices in full, because the temporary rules that capped premiums at 8.5%% of income ended after 2025. Metal level: bronze plans cost less, and in many areas the cheapest gold plan costs about the same as or less than benchmark silver (nationally $694 versus $703 in 2027), so compare before defaulting to silver. County: these are statewide averages, and your county’s benchmark can be well above or below them. Self-employed? Our self-employed health insurance calculator estimates the after-tax cost.

Marketplace Enrollment in Pennsylvania

Pennsylvania residents can enroll in individual and family health insurance plans through Pennie (State-Based). Open enrollment runs November 1 – January 15 (Pennie).

Medicaid in Pennsylvania

Pennsylvania Medicaid (Medical Assistance) expanded in 2015, covering adults with incomes up to 138% of the federal poverty level.

Children's Health Insurance (CHIP) in Pennsylvania

CHIP covers children under 19 in families with incomes up to 314% of the federal poverty level.

What Makes Pennsylvania Unique

Pennsylvania launched Pennie in 2021, transitioning from the federal marketplace to a state-based exchange. Pennie has focused on improving user experience and enrollment assistance. Pennsylvania's marketplace benefits from competition in its major metropolitan areas — Philadelphia and Pittsburgh both have multiple insurers offering plans.

ACA Subsidies: What Pennsylvania Residents Should Know

The enhanced subsidies first enacted in the American Rescue Plan expired at the end of 2025. For 2026, premium tax credits follow the original ACA rules: there is no credit above 400% (the “subsidy cliff”), and enrollees are expected to pay up to 9.96% of income toward the benchmark Silver plan. House in January 2026 but has not become law, so 2027 plans are being priced and subsidized under the same rules unless Congress acts.

New for 2027: premium tax credits are limited to U.S. citizens, green card holders, Cuban/Haitian entrants and COFA migrants. Refugees, asylees, TPS holders and work or student visa holders can still enroll, but at full price — see 2027 ACA subsidies for immigrants.

Pennsylvania’s Marketplace for 2027: Insurers, Rates, and Dates

Pennsylvania residents enroll through Pennie. Full-price premiums rose about 21.5% on average for 2026, before subsidies. 14 insurers sold plans for 2026. Proposed 2027 changes average about 16% before subsidies.

About 501,000 people enrolled for 2026. About 80% received premium tax credits, and the average net premium after credits was about $145 a month.

What’s Different About Health Coverage in Pennsylvania

Pennsylvania runs its own exchange, Pennie, with 14 insurers — one of the most competitive markets in the country. A reinsurance program created under Act 42 (2021) lowers full-price premiums. A state subsidy has been proposed but not funded. Pennsylvania offers easy enrollment through the state tax return.

Tips for Choosing Health Insurance in Pennsylvania

Pennsylvania residents should enroll through Pennie (not HealthCare.gov) to access any state-specific programs and assistance. Medical Assistance Medicaid covers low-income adults. Philadelphia and Pittsburgh benefit from competitive insurer options. CHIP provides children's coverage up to 314% of the federal poverty level.

  • Enroll during open enrollment (November 1 – January 15 (Pennie)) — missing the deadline means waiting until the next open enrollment unless you have a qualifying life event.

Frequently Asked Questions

How many insurers sell marketplace plans in Pennsylvania?

Fourteen insurers sold Pennsylvania marketplace plans for 2026, with proposed 2027 changes averaging about 16% before subsidies.

How much do Pennsylvania marketplace enrollees pay after subsidies?

In 2026 about 80% of Pennsylvania enrollees received premium tax credits, and the average net premium was about $145 a month. There is no credit above 400% of the federal poverty level.

How much does health insurance cost in Pennsylvania in 2027?

Before subsidies, the average benchmark silver plan in Pennsylvania costs $648 a month for a 40-year-old in 2027 (KFF). Using the federal age curve, that is roughly $507 at age 21, $1,376 at age 60 and $2,072 for a family of four with two young children. Households above 400% of the poverty level pay the full price; below that, the premium tax credit lowers it.
