Health Insurance Overview in New Jersey

New Jersey operates Get Covered NJ, a state-based marketplace. New Jersey expanded Medicaid in 2014 and has restored its own individual mandate (with state penalty) after the federal mandate was zeroed out. New Jersey also provides additional state subsidies beyond federal ACA subsidies, making coverage more affordable for middle-income residents. New Jersey has a highly competitive marketplace.

Three things decide what you actually pay. Medicaid in New Jersey

NJ FamilyCare (New Jersey Medicaid) covers adults with incomes up to 138% of the federal poverty level. New Jersey has a strong Medicaid program with comprehensive benefits.

Children's Health Insurance (CHIP) in New Jersey

NJ FamilyCare covers children under 19 in families with incomes up to 350% of the federal poverty level.

What Makes New Jersey Unique

New Jersey restored its individual health insurance mandate in 2019 — one of a handful of states to do so after the federal mandate was effectively eliminated. New Jersey also provides state-funded subsidies for residents with incomes up to 600% of the federal poverty level, making New Jersey one of the most supportive states for middle-income marketplace enrollees.

ACA Subsidies: What New Jersey Residents Should Know

The enhanced subsidies first enacted in the American Rescue Plan expired at the end of 2025. For 2026, premium tax credits follow the original ACA rules: there is no credit above 400% (the “subsidy cliff”), and enrollees are expected to pay up to 9.96% of income toward the benchmark Silver plan. House in January 2026 but has not become law, so 2027 plans are being priced and subsidized under the same rules unless Congress acts.

New for 2027: premium tax credits are limited to U.S. citizens, green card holders, Cuban/Haitian entrants and COFA migrants. Refugees, asylees, TPS holders and work or student visa holders can still enroll, but at full price — see 2027 ACA subsidies for immigrants.

New Jersey’s Marketplace for 2027: Insurers, Rates, and Dates

New Jersey residents enroll through Get Covered New Jersey. Full-price premiums rose about 16.6% on average for 2026, before subsidies. 5 insurers sold plans for 2026. Proposed 2027 changes average about 20.4% before subsidies.

About 509,000 people enrolled for 2026. About 80% received premium tax credits, and the average net premium after credits was about $277 a month.

Open enrollment for 2027: November 1, 2026 through January 31, 2027.

What’s Different About Health Coverage in New Jersey

New Jersey adds its own help, New Jersey Health Plan Savings, for households up to 600% FPL — one of the few places with state assistance above the federal 400% cliff. New Jersey also keeps a state individual mandate, with a penalty on the state tax return for going uninsured without an exemption. Pregnancy is a qualifying event, and Easy Enrollment works through the state tax return.

Tips for Choosing Health Insurance in New Jersey

New Jersey residents should enroll through GetCoveredNJ.gov to access state subsidies for incomes up to 600% of the federal poverty level. New Jersey's extended open enrollment (through January 31) provides additional time. Note New Jersey's individual mandate — uninsured residents may owe a state penalty. Medicaid covers low-income adults.

  • Enroll during open enrollment (November 1 – January 31 (Get Covered NJ — extended deadline)) — missing the deadline means waiting until the next open enrollment unless you have a qualifying life event.

How much do New Jersey marketplace enrollees pay after subsidies?

How much does health insurance cost in New Jersey in 2027?

Using the federal age curve, that is roughly $520 at age 21, $1,412 at age 60 and $2,126 for a family of four with two young children. Households above 400% of the poverty level pay the full price; below that, the premium tax credit lowers it.
