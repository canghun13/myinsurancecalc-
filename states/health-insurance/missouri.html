Health Insurance Overview in Missouri

Missouri expanded Medicaid through a 2020 ballot initiative that voters approved with 53% support. Implementation was delayed by legislative battles but began in 2021. Missouri uses the federal HealthCare.gov marketplace. The expansion covered over 275,000 previously uninsured Missourians and significantly reduced the state's uninsured rate.

Three things decide what you actually pay. Income: below 400%% of the federal poverty level, the premium tax credit cuts the benchmark plan to a set share of income; above it, you pay these prices in full, because the temporary rules that capped premiums at 8.5%% of income ended after 2025. Metal level: bronze plans cost less, and in many areas the cheapest gold plan costs about the same as or less than benchmark silver (nationally $694 versus $703 in 2027), so compare before defaulting to silver. County: these are statewide averages, and your county’s benchmark can be well above or below them. Self-employed? Our self-employed health insurance calculator estimates the after-tax cost.

Medicaid in Missouri

Missouri Medicaid expanded in 2021 following voter approval in 2020. Adults with incomes up to 138% of the federal poverty level are eligible.

Children's Health Insurance (CHIP) in Missouri

MC+ for Kids covers children under 19 in families with incomes up to 300% of the federal poverty level.

What Makes Missouri Unique

Missouri's Medicaid expansion was approved by voters in August 2020 despite strong legislative opposition — the legislature had previously blocked expansion numerous times and initially refused to fund the voter-approved expansion, leading to a court order requiring implementation. Missouri's experience illustrates both the power of direct democracy and legislative resistance to healthcare expansion.

ACA Subsidies: What Missouri Residents Should Know

The enhanced subsidies first enacted in the American Rescue Plan expired at the end of 2025. For 2026, premium tax credits follow the original ACA rules: there is no credit above 400% (the “subsidy cliff”), and enrollees are expected to pay up to 9.96% of income toward the benchmark Silver plan. House in January 2026 but has not become law, so 2027 plans are being priced and subsidized under the same rules unless Congress acts.

New for 2027: premium tax credits are limited to U.S. citizens, green card holders, Cuban/Haitian entrants and COFA migrants. Refugees, asylees, TPS holders and work or student visa holders can still enroll, but at full price — see 2027 ACA subsidies for immigrants.

Missouri’s Marketplace for 2027: Insurers, Rates, and Dates

Missouri residents enroll through HealthCare.gov. Full-price premiums rose about 23.1% on average for 2026, before subsidies. 8 insurers sold plans for 2026; Cox Health Plans (moving off-exchange) is leaving for 2027, so its enrollees need to choose a new plan. Proposed 2027 changes average about 12.5% before subsidies.

About 366,000 people enrolled for 2026. About 87% received premium tax credits, and the average net premium after credits was about $90 a month.

Open enrollment for 2027: November 1, 2026 through January 15, 2027 on HealthCare.gov; enroll by December 15 for coverage starting January 1.

What’s Different About Health Coverage in Missouri

Missouri expanded Medicaid in 2021 after voters approved it, covering adults up to 138% FPL. Its marketplace is competitive, with eight insurers in 2026; Cox Health Plans moves to off-exchange only for 2027, so its enrollees must pick a new plan to keep their tax credit.

Tips for Choosing Health Insurance in Missouri

Missouri residents who were previously uninsured should check Medicaid eligibility following the 2021 expansion. HealthCare.gov subsidies are available for incomes 100-400% of the federal poverty level. MC+ provides children's coverage. Missouri's expansion significantly improved coverage options for low-income adults.

  • Enroll during open enrollment (November 1 – January 15 (federal marketplace)) — missing the deadline means waiting until the next open enrollment unless you have a qualifying life event.

Frequently Asked Questions

Which insurers are leaving the Missouri marketplace for 2027?

Cox Health Plans (moving off-exchange) is leaving for 2027 (see what to do when your insurer leaves). Enrollees with that insurer should choose a new plan during open enrollment; an automatic replacement may have a different network or drug list.

When is open enrollment in Missouri for 2027?

November 1, 2026 through January 15, 2027 on HealthCare.gov; enroll by December 15 for coverage starting January 1.

How much do Missouri marketplace enrollees pay after subsidies?

In 2026 about 87% of Missouri enrollees received premium tax credits, and the average net premium was about $90 a month. There is no credit above 400% of the federal poverty level.

How much does health insurance cost in Missouri in 2027?

Before subsidies, the average benchmark silver plan in Missouri costs $675 a month for a 40-year-old in 2027 (KFF). Using the federal age curve, that is roughly $528 at age 21, $1,433 at age 60 and $2,158 for a family of four with two young children. Households above 400% of the poverty level pay the full price; below that, the premium tax credit lowers it.
