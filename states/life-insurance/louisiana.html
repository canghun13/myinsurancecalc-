Life Insurance in Louisiana: State Regulations

Louisiana is a community property state with a unique legal system based on French civil law rather than English common law. This affects how life insurance policies are treated in the context of marital property and succession. Louisiana has no state income tax on life insurance death benefits. The state has specific laws regarding the rights of forced heirs (children) in succession planning.

What Makes Louisiana Unique

Louisiana's civil law system — inherited from French and Spanish colonial law — creates unique estate planning considerations. Louisiana has forced heirship rules that can affect how life insurance is structured in estate plans. Residents should work with a Louisiana-licensed attorney familiar with civil law succession when incorporating life insurance into estate planning.

Life Insurance After Cancer in Louisiana

Louisiana residents with a cancer history can access life insurance through specialized carriers. Louisiana's civil law succession rules make proper beneficiary designation particularly important for cancer survivors with complex family situations.

Beneficiary Rules and Estate Planning in Louisiana

Louisiana is a community property state with civil law succession rules. Life insurance proceeds pass to named beneficiaries outside of probate, but forced heirship rules may affect estate planning structures. Consulting a Louisiana attorney is strongly recommended for estate planning involving life insurance.

How Much Coverage a Typical Louisiana Household Needs

Louisiana’s median household income was $60,986 in 2024 (U.S. Census Bureau, American Community Survey), 25% below the national median of $81,604. The common rule of thumb of 10 times income puts a typical Louisiana household’s coverage need at roughly $610,000 — before adding a mortgage balance, college costs, or subtracting savings and existing workplace coverage.

Our estimate for a healthy 35-year-old in Louisiana is about $445 a year for a 20-year, $500,000 term policy, which puts Louisiana above the middle of the 50 states in our estimates (median $420).

Community Property and Your Beneficiary in Louisiana

Louisiana is a community property state. A policy bought during marriage with marital earnings is generally community property, so the spouse has an interest in it even if they aren’t the insured or the owner. Naming someone other than your spouse — a child from a prior relationship, a sibling, or a partner — can be challenged by the surviving spouse. If that’s your plan, get your spouse’s written consent when you make the designation, or use a trust. After a divorce, update the beneficiary yourself rather than relying on state default rules.

Frequently Asked Questions

How much life insurance does a typical Louisiana family need?

Using the 10-times-income rule of thumb and Louisiana’s 2024 median household income of $60,986, a typical household would need roughly $610,000, adjusted for mortgage, college costs, savings and existing coverage.

Does Louisiana have an estate or inheritance tax?

What happens to my life insurance if my insurer fails in Louisiana?

Louisiana’s guaranty association covers up to $300,000 in death benefits, $100,000 in cash surrender value and $250,000 in annuity benefits per insolvent insurer.

Is life insurance community property in Louisiana?
