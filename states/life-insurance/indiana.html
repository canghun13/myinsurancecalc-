Life Insurance in Indiana: State Regulations

Indiana regulates life insurance through the Department of Insurance. The state has no income tax on life insurance death benefits. Indiana follows standard industry practices for contestability periods, suicide exclusions, and grace periods. The state has a competitive insurance market with many carriers licensed to operate.

What Makes Indiana Unique

Indiana's manufacturing-heavy economy means many residents have occupational life insurance through union contracts or employer benefits. However, these policies are typically not portable and may not provide adequate coverage. Indiana workers should supplement employer-provided coverage with individual policies that remain in force regardless of employment status.

Life Insurance After Cancer in Indiana

Indiana residents with a cancer history can obtain life insurance through specialized carriers. Indiana University Health's cancer center creates good documentation for the underwriting process.

Beneficiary Rules and Estate Planning in Indiana

Indiana law revokes beneficiary designations to a former spouse upon divorce, providing an automatic protection that not all states offer.

How Much Coverage a Typical Indiana Household Needs

Indiana’s median household income was $71,959 in 2024 (U.S. Census Bureau, American Community Survey), 12% below the national median of $81,604. The common rule of thumb of 10 times income puts a typical Indiana household’s coverage need at roughly $720,000 — before adding a mortgage balance, college costs, or subtracting savings and existing workplace coverage.

Our estimate for a healthy 35-year-old in Indiana is about $410 a year for a 20-year, $500,000 term policy, which puts Indiana below the middle of the 50 states in our estimates (median $420).

How to Save on Life Insurance in Indiana

Indiana manufacturing workers with union-provided life insurance should supplement with individual coverage for portability and adequacy. Term life insurance provides the highest coverage at the lowest cost for young families. Indiana's competitive market makes comparison shopping effective.

  • If you ever have a dispute over a claim or feel you were treated unfairly, you can file a formal complaint with the Indiana Department of Insurance — documented complaints are typically resolved faster than phone calls to the insurer alone.

Frequently Asked Questions

How much life insurance does a typical Indiana family need?

Using the 10-times-income rule of thumb and Indiana’s 2024 median household income of $71,959, a typical household would need roughly $720,000, adjusted for mortgage, college costs, savings and existing coverage.

Does Indiana have an estate or inheritance tax?

Indiana has no state estate or inheritance tax.

What happens to my life insurance if my insurer fails in Indiana?

Indiana’s guaranty association covers up to $300,000 in death benefits, $100,000 in cash surrender value and $250,000 in annuity benefits per insolvent insurer.
