Life Insurance in Arizona: State Regulations

Arizona is a community property state, which has important implications for life insurance ownership and beneficiary designations. A spouse may have community property rights in a life insurance policy purchased with marital funds. Arizona has no state income tax on life insurance death benefits. The state has a competitive life insurance market with many carriers licensed to operate.

What Makes Arizona Unique

Arizona's community property laws mean that a spouse's consent may be required when naming a non-spouse beneficiary on a life insurance policy purchased with community funds. This is a critical planning consideration that Arizona residents should discuss with an attorney or financial advisor when setting up coverage.

Life Insurance After Cancer in Arizona

Arizona residents with a cancer history can obtain life insurance through specialized high-risk carriers or guaranteed issue policies. The time since diagnosis and cancer type significantly affect eligibility.

Beneficiary Rules and Estate Planning in Arizona

Arizona's community property laws require careful beneficiary planning. Life insurance proceeds paid to a named beneficiary pass outside of probate. Community property interests in a policy may require a spouse's consent for certain designations.

How Much Coverage a Typical Arizona Household Needs

Arizona’s median household income was $81,486 in 2024 (U.S. Census Bureau, American Community Survey), about the same as the national median of $81,604. The common rule of thumb of 10 times income puts a typical Arizona household’s coverage need at roughly $810,000 — before adding a mortgage balance, college costs, or subtracting savings and existing workplace coverage.

Our estimate for a healthy 35-year-old in Arizona is about $410 a year for a 20-year, $500,000 term policy, which puts Arizona below the middle of the 50 states in our estimates (median $420).

Community Property and Your Beneficiary in Arizona

Arizona is a community property state. A policy bought during marriage with marital earnings is generally community property, so the spouse has an interest in it even if they aren’t the insured or the owner. Naming someone other than your spouse — a child from a prior relationship, a sibling, or a partner — can be challenged by the surviving spouse. If that’s your plan, get your spouse’s written consent when you make the designation, or use a trust. After a divorce, update the beneficiary yourself rather than relying on state default rules.

Frequently Asked Questions

How much life insurance does a typical Arizona family need?

Using the 10-times-income rule of thumb and Arizona’s 2024 median household income of $81,486, a typical household would need roughly $810,000, adjusted for mortgage, college costs, savings and existing coverage.

Does Arizona have an estate or inheritance tax?

Arizona has no state estate or inheritance tax.

What happens to my life insurance if my insurer fails in Arizona?

Arizona’s guaranty association covers up to $300,000 in death benefits, $100,000 in cash surrender value and $250,000 in annuity benefits per insolvent insurer.

Is life insurance community property in Arizona?

Generally, a policy bought during marriage with marital earnings is community property in Arizona, so the spouse has an interest in it. Naming someone else as beneficiary can be challenged unless the spouse consents in writing.
