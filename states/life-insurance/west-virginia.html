Life Insurance in West Virginia: State Regulations

West Virginia regulates life insurance through the Offices of the Insurance Commissioner. The state has no income tax on life insurance death benefits and no estate tax. West Virginia has higher-than-average rates of chronic health conditions including cardiovascular disease, diabetes, and substance use disorders, all of which affect life insurance underwriting.

What Makes West Virginia Unique

West Virginia has significant rates of opioid addiction and substance use disorders, which can affect life insurance eligibility. Many carriers exclude coverage for death resulting from substance use, and some carriers may decline applicants with recent substance use histories. Applicants with a history of substance use disorders should work with an independent broker familiar with carriers that take a more favorable view of recovery.

Life Insurance After Cancer in West Virginia

West Virginia residents with a cancer history can access life insurance through specialized carriers. WVU Cancer Institute provides treatment documentation.

Beneficiary Rules and Estate Planning in West Virginia

West Virginia has no estate tax.

How Much Coverage a Typical West Virginia Household Needs

West Virginia’s median household income was $60,798 in 2024 (U.S. Census Bureau, American Community Survey), 25% below the national median of $81,604. The common rule of thumb of 10 times income puts a typical West Virginia household’s coverage need at roughly $610,000 — before adding a mortgage balance, college costs, or subtracting savings and existing workplace coverage.

Our estimate for a healthy 35-year-old in West Virginia is about $425 a year for a 20-year, $500,000 term policy, which puts West Virginia above the middle of the 50 states in our estimates (median $420).

Frequently Asked Questions

How much life insurance does a typical West Virginia family need?

Using the 10-times-income rule of thumb and West Virginia’s 2024 median household income of $60,798, a typical household would need roughly $610,000, adjusted for mortgage, college costs, savings and existing coverage.

Does West Virginia have an estate or inheritance tax?

West Virginia has no state estate or inheritance tax.

What happens to my life insurance if my insurer fails in West Virginia?

West Virginia’s guaranty association covers up to $300,000 in death benefits, $100,000 in cash surrender value and $250,000 in annuity benefits per insolvent insurer.
