Life Insurance in Alaska: State Regulations

Alaska regulates life insurance through the Division of Insurance under the Department of Commerce, Community, and Economic Development. The state has no income tax, meaning life insurance death benefits are not subject to state income tax. Alaska's remote communities can face higher costs for medical exams required in underwriting.

What Makes Alaska Unique

Alaska's high cost of living and remote geography make life insurance planning particularly important. A breadwinner's death in a remote Alaskan community can be financially devastating given limited local job markets and the cost of potentially relocating. Higher face value coverage is generally recommended for Alaska families.

Life Insurance After Cancer in Alaska

Life insurance after a cancer diagnosis is available through specialized carriers. No-exam guaranteed issue policies are an option for Alaska residents who cannot pass standard underwriting.

Beneficiary Rules and Estate Planning in Alaska

Alaska is one of only a few states that has adopted a community property system (optional). Life insurance death benefits paid to a named beneficiary pass outside of probate regardless of marital property classification. Beneficiary designations should be reviewed regularly.

How Much Coverage a Typical Alaska Household Needs

Alaska’s median household income was $95,665 in 2024 (U.S. Census Bureau, American Community Survey), 17% above the national median of $81,604. The common rule of thumb of 10 times income puts a typical Alaska household’s coverage need at roughly $960,000 — before adding a mortgage balance, college costs, or subtracting savings and existing workplace coverage.

Our estimate for a healthy 35-year-old in Alaska is about $390 a year for a 20-year, $500,000 term policy, which puts Alaska among the five lowest of the 50 states in our estimates (median $420).

How to Save on Life Insurance in Alaska

Alaska's remote communities can make in-person medical exams difficult — no-exam life insurance policies (which use health questionnaires instead) are particularly useful for rural residents. Online term life insurance shopping is highly effective in Alaska given limited local agent availability. Purchasing coverage young locks in lower rates before Alaska's high-risk outdoor lifestyle factors into premiums.

  • If you ever have a dispute over a claim or feel you were treated unfairly, you can file a formal complaint with the Alaska Division of Insurance — documented complaints are typically resolved faster than phone calls to the insurer alone.

Frequently Asked Questions

How much life insurance does a typical Alaska family need?

Using the 10-times-income rule of thumb and Alaska’s 2024 median household income of $95,665, a typical household would need roughly $960,000, adjusted for mortgage, college costs, savings and existing coverage.

Does Alaska have an estate or inheritance tax?

Alaska has no state estate or inheritance tax.

What happens to my life insurance if my insurer fails in Alaska?

Alaska’s guaranty association covers up to $300,000 in death benefits, $100,000 in cash surrender value and $250,000 in annuity benefits per insolvent insurer.
