Life Insurance in Nebraska: State Regulations

Nebraska regulates life insurance through the Department of Insurance. The state has no income tax on life insurance death benefits. Nebraska has a stable economy with agricultural, finance, and insurance sectors. Omaha is home to several major insurance companies, giving Nebraska residents excellent access to competitive products.

What Makes Nebraska Unique

Omaha is home to several major insurance companies and financial institutions, including Mutual of Omaha and Berkshire Hathaway's insurance operations. Nebraska residents — particularly in Omaha — have exceptional access to competitive life insurance products and sophisticated financial planning resources that would otherwise require travel to larger metropolitan areas.

Life Insurance After Cancer in Nebraska

Nebraska residents with a cancer history can access life insurance through specialized carriers. The University of Nebraska Medical Center provides strong cancer treatment documentation for underwriting.

Beneficiary Rules and Estate Planning in Nebraska

Nebraska law revokes former spouse beneficiary designations upon divorce.

How Much Coverage a Typical Nebraska Household Needs

Nebraska’s median household income was $76,376 in 2024 (U.S. Census Bureau, American Community Survey), 6% below the national median of $81,604. The common rule of thumb of 10 times income puts a typical Nebraska household’s coverage need at roughly $760,000 — before adding a mortgage balance, college costs, or subtracting savings and existing workplace coverage.

Our estimate for a healthy 35-year-old in Nebraska is about $408 a year for a 20-year, $500,000 term policy, which puts Nebraska below the middle of the 50 states in our estimates (median $420).

Life Insurance and Nebraska Death Taxes

Nebraska’s inheritance tax is assessed by counties at 1% for close relatives, 11% for more distant relatives, and 15% for others, after relationship-based exemptions. Life insurance paid to a named beneficiary is generally exempt from the inheritance tax, which makes it one of the more efficient ways to leave money to someone outside the exempt classes. Nebraska has no separate estate tax; the federal estate tax applies only above $15 million per person in 2026.

Frequently Asked Questions

How much life insurance does a typical Nebraska family need?

Using the 10-times-income rule of thumb and Nebraska’s 2024 median household income of $76,376, a typical household would need roughly $760,000, adjusted for mortgage, college costs, savings and existing coverage.

Does Nebraska have an estate or inheritance tax?

Nebraska’s inheritance tax is assessed by counties at 1% for close relatives, 11% for more distant relatives, and 15% for others, after relationship-based exemptions.

What happens to my life insurance if my insurer fails in Nebraska?

Nebraska’s guaranty association covers up to $300,000 in death benefits, $100,000 in cash surrender value and $250,000 in annuity benefits per insolvent insurer.
