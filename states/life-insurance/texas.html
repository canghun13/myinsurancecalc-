Life Insurance in Texas: State Regulations

Texas regulates life insurance through the Department of Insurance. Texas has no state income tax and no estate tax — making it one of the most tax-favorable large states in the nation. Texas has strong asset protection laws for life insurance, with cash value in life insurance policies exempt from creditor claims in most circumstances. Texas's large and competitive insurance market offers many carrier options.

What Makes Texas Unique

Texas's combination of no income tax, no estate tax, and strong creditor protection for life insurance cash value makes Texas one of the most favorable states in the nation for life insurance planning. Business owners and professionals with liability exposure particularly benefit from the creditor protection afforded to life insurance cash value, which is exempt from most creditor claims in Texas.

Beneficiary Rules and Estate Planning in Texas

Texas is a community property state with no income or estate tax. Life insurance purchased with community funds during marriage may involve community property rights. Named beneficiaries receive proceeds outside of probate. Texas's creditor protection for life insurance cash value makes permanent life insurance an attractive asset protection tool.

Community Property and Your Beneficiary Designation

Texas is a community property state, and that changes how a life insurance beneficiary designation works for married people. A policy bought during the marriage with community funds is generally community property, even if only one spouse is the insured and owner. Each spouse has an interest in it.

The practical consequences show up in three common situations:

  • Naming someone other than your spouse. If the premiums came from community funds and you name, say, a sibling or a child from a prior marriage, the surviving spouse can challenge the designation as a “fraud on the community” when the gift is unfair to them. Courts look at the size of the policy relative to the rest of the community estate.
  • Divorce. Under Texas law, a designation of a former spouse is generally ineffective after divorce unless the decree or a new designation says otherwise. Don’t rely on that default — update the beneficiary yourself.
  • A policy bought before marriage is generally separate property, but premiums paid later from community funds can give the community a reimbursement claim.

If you want a policy to go to someone other than your spouse, the clean approach is written spousal consent at the time you name the beneficiary, or ownership by a trust.

Texas Protects Life Insurance From Creditors

Under Chapter 1108 of the Texas Insurance Code, life insurance benefits and cash values are generally exempt from garnishment, attachment, execution, and creditors’ claims, both for the policy owner and the beneficiary. That protection is broader than in many states and is one reason permanent life insurance is used in Texas asset-protection planning for business owners and professionals.

The exemption is not unlimited: premiums paid to defraud creditors can be recovered, and a policy payable to the insured’s own estate can be exposed to the estate’s debts. Name a person or a trust as beneficiary, not “my estate.”

Term or Whole Life in Texas?

Texas sells both, under the same rules as other states. The choice depends on how long you need coverage, not on the state. On this page’s estimates for a healthy 35-year-old, $500,000 of 20-year term costs about $440 a year, while $250,000 of whole life costs about $3,930 a year — roughly 18 times as much per dollar of coverage.

  • Term fits most needs that end: income replacement while children are young, a mortgage, a business loan.
  • Permanent coverage fits needs that don’t end: final expenses, a special-needs dependent, business succession, or estate liquidity. Texas’s creditor protection makes cash value more attractive here than in many states, but it doesn’t change the cost comparison.
  • A DUI or other driving record affects both types equally — see life insurance after a DUI.

How to Save on Life Insurance in Texas

Texas business owners and professionals should consider whole or universal life insurance with cash value as an asset protection strategy — cash value is protected from most creditor claims. Texas's large market and strong competition among carriers make comparison shopping particularly rewarding. Term life insurance remains the most cost-effective option for pure income replacement.

  • If you ever have a dispute over a claim or feel you were treated unfairly, you can file a formal complaint with the Texas Department of Insurance — documented complaints are typically resolved faster than phone calls to the insurer alone.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is life insurance community property in Texas?

A policy bought during marriage with community funds is generally community property, so both spouses have an interest in it. Naming someone other than your spouse can be challenged as a fraud on the community if it is unfair to the surviving spouse. Written spousal consent or trust ownership avoids the problem.

Is life insurance protected from creditors in Texas?

Generally yes. Chapter 1108 of the Texas Insurance Code exempts life insurance benefits and cash values from garnishment, attachment, execution, and creditors’ claims, with exceptions such as premiums paid to defraud creditors. Naming your estate as beneficiary can expose the proceeds to estate debts.

What happens to my Texas life insurance if the insurer goes bankrupt?

The Texas Life and Health Insurance Guaranty Association covers up to $300,000 in death benefits and $100,000 in cash surrender value per life, per insolvent insurer, subject to an overall $300,000 limit for one life with some exceptions.

How much is a $500,000 life insurance policy in Texas?

For a healthy 35-year-old non-smoker, a 20-year, $500,000 term policy is estimated at about $440 a year in Texas. Older applicants, smokers, and people with health conditions pay more.
