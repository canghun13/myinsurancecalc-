Life Insurance in Maine: State Regulations

Maine regulates life insurance through the Bureau of Insurance. The state has no income tax on life insurance death benefits. Maine has a relatively older population, which affects both the need for and cost of life insurance. The state has strong consumer protections and a regulated insurance market.

What Makes Maine Unique

Maine has one of the oldest median ages of any state in the nation, which creates distinct life insurance planning needs. Older Maine residents approaching retirement face different life insurance priorities — focusing more on final expense coverage, legacy planning, and long-term care alternatives — than younger families focused on income replacement.

Life Insurance After Cancer in Maine

Maine residents with a cancer history can access life insurance through specialized carriers. Maine's older population means more residents face this situation — specialized brokers are experienced with cancer underwriting.

Beneficiary Rules and Estate Planning in Maine

Maine law revokes former spouse beneficiary designations upon divorce.

How Much Coverage a Typical Maine Household Needs

Maine’s median household income was $76,442 in 2024 (U.S. Census Bureau, American Community Survey), 6% below the national median of $81,604. The common rule of thumb of 10 times income puts a typical Maine household’s coverage need at roughly $760,000 — before adding a mortgage balance, college costs, or subtracting savings and existing workplace coverage.

Our estimate for a healthy 35-year-old in Maine is about $415 a year for a 20-year, $500,000 term policy, which puts Maine below the middle of the 50 states in our estimates (median $420).

Life Insurance and Maine Death Taxes

Maine has its own estate tax on estates over about $7 million — far below the federal exemption of $15 million per person in 2026. Life insurance you own counts toward your taxable estate even though it passes outside probate, so a home, retirement accounts and a large term policy can push an ordinary family over the Maine threshold. Having the policy owned by an irrevocable life insurance trust (ILIT) from the start keeps the death benefit out of the taxable estate; transferring an existing policy generally requires surviving three years.

How to Save on Life Insurance in Maine

Maine's older demographic means final expense and whole life policies are particularly relevant in addition to term coverage. Portland's growing economy attracts younger residents who benefit from locking in term life insurance rates early. Maine residents planning for estate transfer should consider life insurance as a tax-efficient legacy tool.

  • If you ever have a dispute over a claim or feel you were treated unfairly, you can file a formal complaint with the Maine Bureau of Insurance — documented complaints are typically resolved faster than phone calls to the insurer alone.

Frequently Asked Questions

How much life insurance does a typical Maine family need?

Using the 10-times-income rule of thumb and Maine’s 2024 median household income of $76,442, a typical household would need roughly $760,000, adjusted for mortgage, college costs, savings and existing coverage.

Does Maine have an estate or inheritance tax?

Maine has its own estate tax on estates over about $7 million — far below the federal exemption of $15 million per person in 2026.

What happens to my life insurance if my insurer fails in Maine?

Maine’s guaranty association covers up to $300,000 in death benefits, $100,000 in cash surrender value and $250,000 in annuity benefits per insolvent insurer.
