Seller Insurance Requirements by Marketplace, Compared

8 min read · Updated November 2026

Online marketplaces fall into two groups. A few require a certificate of insurance once you reach a trigger — a sales level, a product category, or simply an invitation to sell. The rest require nothing, but leave you fully exposed if a product injures someone. This table is the starting point; each row links to the detail where we have it.

“Not Required” Doesn’t Mean “Not Liable”

Etsy, eBay and Shopify don’t ask for insurance, but product liability law doesn’t care which website a product was sold on. Anyone in the chain of distribution — manufacturer, importer, brand owner, retailer — can be named in an injury suit. If you import products under your own brand, you are often treated as the manufacturer, because the overseas factory is hard for a U.S. plaintiff to reach. Handmade sellers face the same exposure for candles, cosmetics, children’s items and food. The marketplace’s own buyer protection programs cover refunds, not injuries.

Building One Policy for Every Channel

Most multi-channel sellers are better off with a single commercial general liability policy that includes products-completed operations coverage and lists every channel, than with separate policies per platform. To pass all of the requirements above at once, ask for:

A practical way to decide whether a product line can carry its insurance cost is premium per unit: divide the annual premium by the units you sell across all channels, and compare it with your contribution margin. A $1,200 policy is $2.40 a unit at 500 units a year but $0.12 at 10,000. Our Amazon guide has a per-unit table, and the business insurance calculator gives a general liability starting point.

Other Coverage Online Sellers Ask About

  • Product recall insurance pays recall costs such as notification, shipping and destruction, which liability policies exclude.
  • Inventory (commercial property) coverage protects stock in your own space; inventory in an Amazon or third-party warehouse may need a policy that covers property in transit and at others’ premises.
  • Cyber liability matters if you store customer data on your own site.
  • Professional or E&O coverage is worth considering for instructional, wellness or software products where a claim could be about advice rather than injury.

Frequently Asked Questions

Which online marketplaces require sellers to have insurance?

Amazon, Walmart Marketplace and Target Plus require liability insurance once their triggers are met. Amazon requires $1 million coverage for any seller with a listing in an enhanced safety category from November 2, 2026, and for other sellers after a $10,000 month. Walmart requires it above $100,000 in sales over 12 months. TikTok Shop does not currently require it from every seller, and Etsy, eBay and Shopify do not require it.

Does Etsy require sellers to have liability insurance?

No. Etsy does not require insurance, but sellers can still be sued if a product causes injury, and some retailers and wholesale buyers ask Etsy sellers for a certificate of insurance before stocking their products.

Does TikTok Shop require product liability insurance?

Not for every seller as of fall 2026. TikTok has said insurance may become required and that sellers will be notified before enforcement, and it can ask individual sellers for proof of coverage, particularly in higher-risk categories.

Can one insurance policy cover Amazon, Walmart and my own website?

Yes. A single general liability policy with products-completed operations coverage, $1 million per occurrence and $2 million aggregates, a blanket additional insured endorsement and all sales channels disclosed can satisfy both Amazon and Walmart, with a separate certificate issued for each.

How much insurance does Target Plus require?

Seller integration guides cite $5 million of commercial general liability for Target Plus partners, which can be met with a primary policy plus an umbrella or excess policy. Target Plus is invitation-only, so confirm the exact terms in the partner agreement.
