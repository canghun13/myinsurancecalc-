New Jersey E-Bike Insurance Requirement: Who Needs It and What Policy Counts

7 min read · Updated October 2026

New Jersey is one of the first states to require insurance for e-bikes. A law signed in January 2026 treats e-bikes kept in New Jersey as motorized bicycles, with a six-month phase-in that ended on July 19, 2026. Since then, riders need a license, every e-bike needs to be registered with the Motor Vehicle Commission, and faster e-bikes need liability insurance.

The rules were written quickly, the MVC and the Department of Banking and Insurance (DOBI) were still building processes when they took effect, and some local police posted guidance that turned out to be wrong. What follows reflects reporting from Insurance Journal, county traffic-safety offices and insurers as of fall 2026; check the MVC and DOBI for changes.

Registration fees were waived for 2026; an $8 annual fee applies from 2027 according to Atlantic County’s summary. Rental businesses must carry liability coverage regardless of bike speed.

Riders 15 and 16, and anyone without a driver’s license, need a motorized bicycle license, which involves ID verification, a knowledge test, a vision test, and a road test after a permit period. Children under 15 can’t ride e-bikes at all under the law.

These are low limits. A serious injury to a pedestrian can easily exceed $15,000, and the rider is personally responsible for the rest. Most specialty policies offer much higher liability limits for little extra, and it’s worth taking one.

Why Your Homeowners, Renters or Auto Policy Doesn’t Satisfy It

Reporting on the law and insurers’ own guidance both say standard homeowners and auto policies don’t meet the requirement. The reasons go beyond limits:rsquo; own guidance both say standard homeowners and auto policies don’t meet the requirement. The reasons go beyond limits:

  • Auto policies cover the cars listed on them. An e-bike isn’t a listed auto, and a personal auto policy won’t issue an insurance ID card for it.
  • Homeowners and renters policies usually exclude “motor vehicles.” In the standard homeowners form, the main exceptions are for motorized vehicles that aren’t required to be registered for public roads. Now that New Jersey requires e-bike registration, that exclusion is more likely to apply — to liability and to theft or damage of the bike itself. Some insurers write e-bikes back in by endorsement; many don’t.
  • Umbrella policies generally sit over an underlying motor vehicle policy; without one for the e-bike, the umbrella may not respond either.

Even outside New Jersey, check your homeowners or renters policy wording before assuming an e-bike is covered. Theft of a pedal bicycle is ordinary personal property; a motorized bike may not be.

Where to Buy It and What It Costs

As of the July 2026 deadline, Velosurance and Markel were offering policies built for the requirement, a Markel-backed product through the broker Voom was launching, and Progressive said an offering was coming. DOBI said it was reviewing insurer filings and hoped to publish a list of approved carriers. Velosurance quotes specialty e-bike coverage — liability plus theft, damage and medical — at roughly $75–$200 a year, and offers liability limits up to $100,000.

When comparing, check four things: that the policy is designed to meet New Jersey’s motorized-bicycle requirement and issues an ID card; the liability limit (take more than the minimum); whether theft is covered when the bike is locked outside; and whether battery fires at home are covered under the e-bike policy or left to your homeowners or renters policy.

Battery Fires: The Home Insurance Question

Lithium-ion battery fires are the e-bike risk landlords and insurers worry about most. Fire is a covered peril under homeowners and renters policies, so damage to your home and belongings from a battery fire is usually covered even if the bike itself isn’t — but some insurers have contested claims involving modified or non-certified batteries, and some leases now restrict indoor charging. Use a battery and charger certified to UL 2849 or UL 2271, don’t charge overnight unattended, and tell your insurer about the bike. If you rent, check your lease; our renters insurance calculator helps size liability coverage, which is what pays if a fire spreads to a neighbor’s unit.

Quick Checklist for New Jersey Riders

  1. Find out whether your e-bike is low-speed pedal-assist or capable of 20 mph+ under power or throttle.
  2. Register it with the MVC.
  3. Make sure you have a driver’s license or a motorized bicycle license.
  4. If it’s the faster kind, buy a policy that meets the 15/30/5 + PIP minimum — ideally with higher liability limits.
  5. Carry your license, registration and insurance card, and wear a helmet.

Frequently Asked Questions

Do I need insurance for my e-bike in New Jersey?

If your e-bike can reach 20 mph or more under power, typically throttle e-bikes, yes. New Jersey's 2026 law requires liability insurance for those motorized bicycles, with reported minimums of $15,000 per person and $30,000 per accident for bodily injury, $5,000 for property damage, and PIP for pedestrians. Low-speed pedal-assist e-bikes must be registered but do not need insurance, according to reporting on the law.

Does homeowners insurance cover an e-bike in New Jersey?

Standard homeowners and auto policies do not satisfy New Jersey's e-bike insurance requirement. Homeowners policies generally exclude motor vehicles that are required to be registered, so now that New Jersey requires e-bike registration, the exclusion may apply to both liability and theft of the bike. Some insurers add coverage by endorsement; check your policy.

When did New Jersey's e-bike law take effect?

The law was signed in January 2026 with a six-month phase-in. Riders had to comply with the registration, licensing and insurance requirements by July 19, 2026.

How much is e-bike insurance in New Jersey?

Specialty insurers quote e-bike policies covering liability plus theft, damage and medical at roughly $75 to $200 a year. Liability-only policies built for the New Jersey requirement were launching from several insurers in mid-2026; prices depend on limits and the bike's value.

How old do you have to be to ride an e-bike in New Jersey?

At least 15. Riders 17 and older can use a regular driver's license; riders 15 and 16, and anyone without a driver's license, need a motorized bicycle license from the MVC. All riders must wear a helmet.
